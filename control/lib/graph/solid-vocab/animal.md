Mint an ANIMAL — a real quadruped or biped, standing, balanced and grounded, rendered to a sketch. This is the animal REALM of the figure system, not a separate creature generator: it is the SAME manji armature the human figure rides, with the spine reoriented HORIZONTAL, so the flesh model, the proto-skull, the proto-foot, the coat pelage and the mesher are all shared with the human protoform. Reach for it on framing like "a wolf", "a horse", "a deer with antlers", "make me a dog", "a four-legged creature", "a T. rex".

Persisted with kind `animal` — a RECIPE, not a render: `{ archetype, opts, view, elev, crop }` regenerated by the kernel on every read. This is a render you look at: mint it, open the URL (or rasterize the SVG) to SEE it, then adjust knobs. **Iterate in place with `update_sketch` on the same ref** — do not re-mint to change a knob.

For an ARTHROPOD (insect, spider, scorpion, crab, woodlouse, centipede, millipede) see "Arthropods" at the end: they
are assembled from shared parts, not taken from a species list.

For a stylized, invented, non-anatomical creature (a part-graph of bars and lathes), reach for the `manji-tree` kind instead. This kind is for animals with a real skeleton's proportions.

## The two doors

The difference is the BODY MODEL, not the species list. Both accept `opts` on top, so a species is a STARTING POINT, not a fixed thing.

- **`species`** — a dressed recipe on the HERO path: one welded single skin (a marched signed-distance field, so limbs melt into the body with concave fillets) plus a welded skull, dressed with coat / countershading / markings / face / tail. This is what you want when the ask names a real animal.
- **`archetype`** — the bare body on the cheap OVERLAP path: each bone, joint and skull is its own ring-stack shell, depth-sorted. Reads as a clear silhouette in a fraction of the time, but shows seams and carries a separate loose jaw. The lineup / fast-iteration door.

Pass `species` alone, `archetype` alone, or both (an explicit archetype overrides the species' own — e.g. a wolf recipe's coat and face on the `ursine` frame).

## Spec shape

`title`, `ref`, and `folder_ref` are passed at the mint's top level. Everything below goes in `spec`.

```
spec: {
  species?:   'wolf'|'fox'|'camel'|'kangaroo'|'redPanda'|'deer'|'buck'|'gazelle'|
              'lion'|'cougar'|'hippo'|'rhino'|'horse'|'ram'|'bull'|'wombat',
  archetype?: 'rodent'|'canine'|'feline'|'stumpy'|'equine'|'gazelle'|'sauropod'|
              'theropod'|'raptor'|'avian'|'ursine'|'raccoon',
  opts?:      { …build knobs, merged over the species' own — see below },
  view?:      'frontal'|'three-quarter'|'lateral'|'left'|'back' | <azimuth°>,
  elev?:      <camera elevation°, -89…89; default 12>,
  crop?:      'head',          // a face study close-up instead of the whole animal
  background?: <boolean>
}
```

### archetype — the twelve bodies

Each is a proportion set (back height, trunk length, neck, limb angulation, girths) plus its family's skull, foot and stance:

| archetype | reads as | foot | stance |
|---|---|---|---|
| `rodent` | mouse / rat / small gnawer — big head for the body | paw | quadruped |
| `canine` | dog / wolf / fox — elongated muzzle, springy limbs | paw | quadruped |
| `feline` | cat / lion / cougar — short flat face, low crouch | paw | quadruped |
| `stumpy` | hippo / wombat / graviportal — heavy, low, blocky | pad | quadruped |
| `equine` | horse — long deep head, long straight legs | hoof | quadruped |
| `gazelle` | deer / antelope — delicate tapered head, thin legs | cloven hoof | quadruped |
| `sauropod` | long-necked dinosaur — tiny head, columnar legs | pad | quadruped |
| `theropod` | T. rex — huge deep skull, powerful jaw | talon | **biped** (`tuck`) |
| `raptor` | small pointed predator dinosaur | talon | **biped** (`tuck`) |
| `avian` | bird — small cranium, long beak, no jaw | talon | **biped** (`wing`) |
| `ursine` | bear — broad domed skull, boxiest snout | paw | quadruped |
| `raccoon` | small carnivoran, long pointy conical muzzle | paw | quadruped |

Bipeds (`theropod` / `raptor` / `avian`) stand on the two hind feet and have their feet placed under the centre of mass; everything else stands on all four.

### opts — the build knobs

Merged one level deep over the species' own, so `{ skullCfg: { length: 0.3 } }` retunes ONE knob instead of replacing the species' whole skull.

- `skin` — the welded single skin + welded skull (the hero path). Three values, and the choice decides whether the animal can PRINT:
  - `true` — the MARCHED skin: the field ray-marched per axis into open ring tubes. Fast, and what a `species` sets by default. Render-only (see Honest limits).
  - `'watertight'` — the SAME field surfaced by the surface-net polygonizer instead: one closed quad mesh, zero boundary edges by construction. Heavier to build (it samples the whole field volume) and the one to pass when the animal is headed for a printer or an engine. `fleshCfg.cells` sets its grid resolution (default 72).
  - omitted — no weld: each bone and the skull stay separate shells, and the jaw reads loose.
- `armatureCfg` — proportions, scaled or biased off the archetype without minting a new one: `backHeight`, `trunkLength`, `neckLength`, `neckAngle`, `girthBody` / `girthFore` / `girthHind` / `girthHead`, `foreMode` (`ground` | `tuck` | `wing`).
- `skullCfg` — the family tell: `length` (back→snout), `width` (cranium breadth), `dome` (forehead height), `muzzle` (snout fraction of length), `snout` (nose-tip thinness), `muzzleDrop` (how the snout dips), `jaw` (lower-jaw depth, 0 = none), `beak` (beak length, avian), `boxy` (0 conical → 1 squared-off block), `pad` (whisker pads — a wide squared cat snout), `bridge` (a raised nasal ridge), `capDepth` (front-cap roundness; <1 = flatter, squarer nose).
- `fleshCfg` — the body mass: `thorax`, `belly`, `bellyDrop`, `rump`, `haunch` (the hindquarter "ham"), `taper`, `blend` (joint fillet softness), `N` / `M` (skin resolution). Two knobs apply to `skin: 'watertight'` only:
  - `cells` — surface-net grid resolution along the longest side (default 72). Raises the polygon count, and is what smooths the SILHOUETTE.
  - `smooth` (boolean, default off) — shade per VERTEX instead of per face. The body field is analytic, so each corner gets its exact normal rather than an average — the faceting inside the form goes away at the SAME polygon count. Free to ask for, changes no geometry, and the print file is byte-identical either way. It does not touch the outline: only `cells` does that.
- `footCfg` — `kind` (`paw` | `hoof` | `pad` | `talon`), `cloven`, plus per-kind sizes (`topR`, `width`, `fwd`, `padH`, `toeR`, `hoofR`, `padR`, `ankleH`, `spread`).
- `coat` — the coat COLOUR, painted onto the body and head (`color`). Not per-strand geometry: strand fur exists only on the banded ringtail (`tailRings`). `underHex` + `underCut` countershade the DOWN-facing faces (a pale belly); `overHex` + `overCut` do the same for UP-facing faces (a dorsal stripe, skunk/badger).
- `face` — eyes / nose / ear decor off a preset; `facePaint` — a muzzle mask; `plateMuzzle` — a projecting four-plate snout off a small cranium.
- `mane` (lion), `antlers` (deer/buck), `tailCfg` / `tailTip` / `tailRings` / `tailBands` (a brush, a dark tip, a banded ringtail), `fluffs`, `forepaws`.

### view / elev / crop

`view` is the camera azimuth: `frontal` (head-on, 180°), `three-quarter` (130°, the default), `lateral` (90°, the silhouette read), `left`, `back`, or a raw number. `elev` is the camera elevation in degrees (default 12 — slightly above). `crop: 'head'` renders a FACE STUDY: only the head-region parts, framed close.

A silhouette reads truest at `lateral`; the face reads truest at `frontal` or `three-quarter` with `crop: 'head'`. Render more than one before judging a species — a head that reads well at one azimuth can be wrong from another.

## Honest limits

- **Print lane — depends on `skin`.** With `skin: true` (or omitted) the mesh is RENDER-ONLY geometry: open ring-strip tubes assembled by overlap, hidden by painter's sort and back-face culling, and an STL closure audit (`export_model`) reports it not closed (a canine: 864 boundary edges). With `skin: 'watertight'` the audit passes — zero significant holes — though a small number of sub-threshold rims remain at self-closing pole tips, which the audit does not count as defects. Either verdict is advisory, never a refusal: suitability is the operator's call.
- **Coat surface:** the countershade and marking rules are per-face normal tests, so a coat can read as flat hard-edged patches rather than blended fur. `skin: 'watertight'` + `fleshCfg.smooth` softens the shading within each zone; the ZONE BOUNDARIES stay hard-edged either way, because they are decided per face on purpose. Structure is the strong half; surface is the developing one.
- The bare (`archetype`-only) path draws its lower jaw as a separate part, which can read as detached from some angles. Pass `skin: true` — or a `species` — for a welded head.

## Arthropods (`bug`)

Arthropods are not a species list. They are ASSEMBLED from shared PART FORMS (lib/graph/bugs), and two bugs share a
part by naming the same form: the bee and the house fly stand on the same `walker` leg; the grasshopper, a flea
beetle and a leafhopper wear the same `jumper` hind leg. The body is a HEAD (or `fused`), a TRUNK of repeated
segments each carrying leg pairs (an insect: three of one pair; a spider: one of four; a crab: one of five, the
first the chelipeds; a centipede: fifteen of one; a millipede: dozens of two), and a TAIL. Legs are bent manji
chains of pieces (carrot podomeres, chili claws, banana chela fingers) whose feet are solved onto the ground; size
is exact by construction (the body is measured and rebuilt at its published length, head front to tail tip).

```
spec: {
  species?: <a worked bug>,     // honeyBee, houseFly, grasshopper, ladybird, … (the error lists the current roster);
                                // or the name people say ('a ladybug', 'crawdad'), or one not built ('wasp': its
                                // stand-in, echoed back in `resolved_from`)
  bug?: {                       // ONE NOBODY HAS BUILT: the closest worked bug, the asked part forms worn over it
    order?:  'Hymenoptera'|'Formicidae'|'Diptera'|'Culicidae'|'Coleoptera'|'Curculionidae'|'Lepidoptera'|
             'Orthoptera'|'Hemiptera'|'Cicadidae'|'Neuroptera'|'Ephemeroptera'|'Odonata'|'Mantodea'|'Phasmida'|
             'Dermaptera'|'Blattodea'|'Siphonaptera'|'Araneae'|'Opiliones'|'Ixodida'|'Scorpiones'|'Xiphosura'|
             'Brachyura'|'Astacidea'|'Achelata'|'Chilopoda'|'Diplopoda'|'Isopoda',   // a prior: the forms that order usually wears
    like?:   <a worked bug>,                 // force the basis instead of matching
    traits?: { head, trunk, tail, legs, foreLegs, hindLegs, antennae, mouth, eyes, palps,
               wings: [forms] | null, wingPose, extras: [kinds] },   // part FORM names (below)
    length?: <metres, head front to tail tip>, name?, colors?: { body, head, trunk, tail, legs, eyes, wing, elytra, … },
    over?:   { <any bauplan field> },        // raw overrides, e.g. { legs: { reach: 1.3 } }
  },
}
```

Every worked bug has an ENCYCLOPEDIA ENTRY (generated, card id `species/<id>`: its names, scientific name, published
size and source, and starters to copy), and each class a hub (`species/insects`, `species/arachnids`,
`species/crustaceans`, `species/myriapods`) naming what is not built yet and what stands in. Search finds them by the
everyday name; read the entry before minting. Sizes are true scale: scale up (`bug: { like, length }`) for a print.

Forms (the common denominators; lib/graph/bugs/forms.js documents every field):
- head: `hypognathous` (face down) · `prognathous` (face forward) · `opisthognathous` (beak back) · `globe` · `fused`
- trunk: `compact` · `shield` · `saddle` · `slim` · `long` · `slanted` · `cephalothorax` · `prosoma` · `carapace` ·
  `cylinder` · `centipede` · `millipede` · `isopod`
- tail: `oval` · `tapered` · `long` · `gaster` (with a waist) · `sac` · `flat` · `telson` · `pleon` · `mesosoma` ·
  `tucked` · `muscle`
- legs: `walker` · `runner` · `jumper` · `grasper` · `digger` · `swimmer` · `stilt` · `spider` · `tucked` · `manyfoot` ·
  `crabwalker` · `cheliped` · `palp`
- antennae: `filiform` · `setaceous` · `whip` · `moniliform` · `clavate` · `capitate` · `geniculate` · `pectinate` ·
  `plumose` · `lamellate` · `aristate` · `peduncle`
- mouth: `mandibles` · `tusks` · `coiled` · `stylet` · `beak` · `sponge` · `snout` · `fangs` · `forcipules` · `none`
- eyes: `compound` · `large` · `holoptic` · `small` · `simple` · `stalked` · `none`
- wings: `membrane` · `scaled` · `scaledHind` · `narrow` · `tegmen` · `haltere` · `elytra`; poses `spread` · `flat` ·
  `roof` · `upright` · `vee`
- extras: `cerci` · `sting` · `ovipositor` · `filament` · `metasoma` (a scorpion's tail) · `fan` (a crustacean's)

Opt-in knobs (in `over`, or on a worked bug's plan): a `neck` section and trunk `pitch`; leg `turn` (a joint turned
out of the leg's plane), per-pair socket `at`, forelegs `on: 'neck'`; section `belly` (a flat underside) and
`samplesPerSeg` (thin bands); jaw `pitch` / `teeth`; wing `slots` / `stations` with `veins` markings; elytra `seam`;
markings on legs (`on: 'legs'`).

The mint returns `stance` (hexapod, octopod, decapod, isopod, myriapod), `legs`, `length_m`, and for a `bug`: `basis`
(the worked bug it started from), `worn` (each part swapped) and `ranked` (the three closest). The result is a
layered ring plan: tune it in place with `update_sketch` on `/plan/...`. Two gates, never conflated: the machine gate
(`node scripts/fauna-fit.mjs --bug <id>`: closed, grounded, every foot planted, size) and the eyes gate, the operator's.
