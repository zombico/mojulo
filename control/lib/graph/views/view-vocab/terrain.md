A WORLD at real scale: ground in metres that you walk (1.7 m eye, walking pace), fly (speed grows with height above the ground) and pull back from to see the whole thing. It is made one of two ways: from a painted landscape (`from`), or composed from named features (`world`) whose real sizes set its scale. Either can carry `cities`. From a painting, the painting's own terrain comes along: its heartbeat or `elevation`, its `landform` (peaks, strata, scarps, joints, talus), its `erosion`, its palette and its stone. Below the painting's grid, a fracture-rough detail law carries the ground down to footstep scale, rougher on bare rock than on soil. The World page carries the RECIPE, not a mesh, and builds the terrain around the camera: fine near, coarse far, capped in memory. Exports (GLB, USD, 3MF, STL, the engines) and stills get a baked mesh of the whole world. Served at `/api/sketches/<ref>/world`; open it and use walk / fly (WASD, Space, Shift) or drag to orbit. The bookmarks are `ground`, `aerial` and `world`, plus `planet` with `planet` set.

## Parameters

Pass these via `compose_world`'s `overrides`. `seed`, `title`, `ref`, `folder_ref` are top-level `compose_world` params.

- `from` (object; this or `world`) — the painted landscape the world is made from: an inline painted-landscape recipe (`{ heartbeat | elevation, splatch, landform?, erosion?, rocks?, seed? }`, see `get_view_vocab({ id: 'painted-landscape' })`), or `{ ref: '<painted-landscape sketch>' }` to promote a stored one. A ref follows its source: edit the painting, and the world changes with it.
- `span` (number, metres; default 2400) — how many metres the painting's width covers. Heights scale by the same factor, so a 4-unit cliff in the painting stands 400 m at the default. A smaller span gives a valley; a larger one gives a range.
- `relief` (number; default 1) — vertical exaggeration on top of the span's scale.
- `horizon` (`'plain'` default | `'sea'` | `'none'`) — how the ground continues past the painting: rolling land eased down from its edges, a sea (the painting's `waterLevel` or its low ground), or nothing (the world ends at its edge).
- `planet` (`true` or `{ radius }`, metres; default radius 8 × span) — wrap the world onto a small planet you can fly up from and see whole from orbit. The painting sits at the north pole, undistorted. Past it, continents and seas run on around the globe, lit by the painting's sun, so there is a night side. High up, the sky gives way to space and a rim of air; the `planet` bookmark looks at it from three radii out. Walk stays near the painting: gravity is straight down, true there and increasingly false far across the globe. Scree and `place` stand on the globe's ground; `cities`, `plants` and `grass` are for flat worlds.
- `detail` (`false` or `{ rock?, soil?, hurst?, crossover?, min? }`, metres) — the footstep-scale roughness. It defaults from the painting's grid: a few metres on bare rock, a fraction of that on soil, Hurst 0.8, from four grid cells down to 0.5 m. `false` gives the grid's smooth facets.
- `spawn` ([x, y], metres) — where the walk starts. Default: the painting's foreground, looking into it.
- `place` (list) — other sketches stood on the ground: `{ ref, at: [x, y], size?: metres | [w, d] (20), height?, facing?: 'N'|'E'|'S'|'W', turn?, sink? }`. Each is fitted into its footprint and seated at the lowest ground under it, less `sink`. A castle on a slope digs its uphill side into the hill instead of floating.
- `world` (object; this or `from`) — compose the world from features instead of painting it: `{ features: [{ feature, size?, side?, along?, at?, length? }…], climate?, focus?, seed? }`. The FIRST feature is the anchor and its size sets the world's span, so ask for the thing and the scale follows. At most 8 features. `span`, `relief`, `horizon` and `detail` do not apply. Features and their sizes (default first; real bands):
  - `river`: `river` (150–600 km long, 60–250 m wide at the mouth), `stream` (5–30 km, 3–12 m), `great` (1,500–4,000 km, 0.8–2 km: a continent). It runs from high ground (a range's flank when there is one) to the coast, the lake it feeds, or the frame's edge, through any lake declared after it. Its water never climbs, and it cuts a valley through whatever it crosses.
  - `range`: `range` (150–500 km, peaks 2–3.5 km), `ridge` (10–40 km, 0.4–1 km), `cordillera` (1,500–5,000 km, 4–6.5 km). `along`: `NS`, `EW`, `NE` or `NW`.
  - `lake`: `lake` (15–60 km), `tarn` (0.3–1.5 km), `great` (150–550 km: an inland sea). A lake anchor drains by an outlet river; a lake after a river sits on its course.
  - `plateau`: `plateau` (20–80 km, 0.3–1.2 km high), `mesa` (1–8 km), `tableland` (200–600 km). `volcano`: `volcano` (20–40 km across, 2–4 km high), `cone` (2–6 km), `shield` (80–150 km).
  - `coast`: `side` `N`, `E`, `S` or `W` becomes sea past a fractal shoreline.

  `length` (metres) overrides a feature's band. `at` ([x, y], fractions of the frame, −0.5…0.5) pins where it goes; otherwise each takes the place the anchor gives it or the one that overlaps the rest least. `climate`: `temperate` (default), `arid`, `alpine`, `tropical`, `boreal` (colours, snowline, treeline). Rivers and lakes come from drainage and erosion around what was declared; the plan is finest (≈ 16 m, then fracture detail to half a metre) around `focus` ([x, y] metres; default: the anchor's showcase, a great river's lower course, a lake's shore, a range's valley floor), and coarser toward the edges of a continent. The walk starts there, on dry ground. Bookmarks: `ground`, `aerial`, `region`, `world` (and `planet`). With `planet`, a continent defaults to Earth's radius.
- `cities` (list) — fractal cities that stand on the ground: `{ at?: [x, y], size?: metres | [w, d], profile?: 'city'|'town'|'metro', seed?, grade?: { max?, cliff? }, detail?: { radius }, …any fractal-city recipe field }` or `{ ref: '<fractal-city sketch>', at? }`. At most 6 cities, their footprints totalling at most 8 km². Omit `at` and it is sited on the flattest dry ground with water in reach (on a composed world, near the focus); on a wide painted world (a small city on a span of more than about 8 km) the search is refused, so give it an `at`. Ground too steep to build on (a datum over `grade.max`, 20 % by default), cliffs, scree and water are reserved before the streets are laid, so the streets go round them. The city stands on the land smoothed at half a block; each building stands level at its front door, with a stone plinth where the ground falls away; towers keep to the flat. A city wider than 600 m (metro sprawl) keeps full detail within `detail.radius` (200 m) of its core and is massing beyond it, its streets painted into the ground. The walk starts on its main street; bookmarks `city` and `city-aerial`.
- `plants` (`true` or `{ radius?, level?, variants?, figs?, region? }`; composed worlds, flat) — grown plants where the ground is painted wood, built by the page around the camera. The climate is two dials: a temperature that falls with altitude (pinned to the climate's treeline) and a moisture that rises near water. Its species by zone: temperate oak and beech low, fir toward the treeline; alpine beech and fir; boreal fir; arid date palms and Washingtonia along the rivers; tropical coconuts on the low coast, Bambusa clumps along rivers, and a mountain forested to its treeline (the umbrella tree `schefflera` low, tree ferns and Moso in the cloud belt, fir under the treeline); reeds on a lake's or a river's shore everywhere. No plant stands on water, sand, bare rock, snow or a city's graded ground, and trees shorten toward the treeline. Within `radius` (1200 m) each plant takes the detail its size on screen earns, within a draw budget, down to a far level of fourteen triangles; past it the ground's own colour carries the woods. `level` (`'L2'`) caps the detail, `variants` (2) the grown variants per species; `figs: true` adds sparse banyans, strangler figs and rubber figs to a tropical world's lowland forest. Exports carry the plants within 600 m of the spawn. `region: 'eurasia'` grows the region's own conifers in place of fir: boreal Norway spruce throughout with Scots pine on the ground away from water; temperate oak and beech low (alpine beech alone), silver fir with beech in the montane belt, spruce to the treeline, and pine on dry ground. Set it when a person names northern or central Europe or Siberia (Scandinavia, the Alps, the Carpathians, the taiga). Known gap: arid scrub stays painted.
- `grass` (`true` or `{ radius?, near?, density?, cover?, kinds?, variants?, style? }`; composed worlds, flat) — grass stands around the camera where the ground is open: in clumps (drifts of a sward, knots of tussocks) with open ground between, patchy, thinning under trees and never on water, sand, bare rock, snow or a city's graded ground. The climate picks the kinds by temperature and moisture (temperate `meadow`, `fescue`, `sedge`; `tussock` toward and above the treeline; arid `needlegrass`; tropical `elephant` grass in the lowland); `kinds` names them instead, from `fescue`, `meadow`, `tussock`, `needlegrass`, `sedge`, `fountain`, `pampas`, `elephant`, and `lawn` (a short sward: plugs of turf packed into a carpet, seen only near). `style: 'stylized'` draws any kind in a stylized game's look: chunky standing blades in a saturated dark-to-bright gradient, lit as the ground is, denser (`density` 5). Within `radius` (60 m) every tuft stands inside its own `near` (12 m, longer for taller kinds); past it they thin with distance, the survivors wider and drawn toward the ground's colour, so the far meadow is the ground. `density` (3.5 tufts a square metre of meadow), `cover` (0.45, the share of open ground in meadow). Live World page only; exports carry no grass.
- `lod` (`{ minSize?, split?, maxChunks? }`) — the page's terrain budget. Defaults: the finest chunk is 32 m (1 m between vertices), split 1.6, at most 520 chunks (about 15 MB).

The world frame is metres, z up. The painting's centre is the origin, its width runs along x (±span/2), and its near side (the painting's foreground) is +y. The painting's talus scree comes along as instanced boulders.

## Recipes

- A big river: `overrides: { world: { features: [{ feature: 'river', size: 'great' }, { feature: 'coast', side: 'S' }, { feature: 'range' }] } }` — a continent: you stand on a kilometre-wide river's bank; fly up to see it cross to the sea.
- A huge lake: `overrides: { world: { features: [{ feature: 'lake', size: 'great' }], climate: 'boreal' } }`. A cordillera by the sea: `features: [{ feature: 'range', size: 'cordillera', along: 'NS' }, { feature: 'coast', side: 'W' }]`.
- A river town: add `cities: [{ profile: 'town' }]` to either. A metro on the hills: `cities: [{ profile: 'metro', size: [2400, 1600] }]` on a painted world with `span: 8000`.

- Promote a painting: `compose_world({ base: 'terrain', overrides: { from: { ref: '<painted-landscape>' } } })`.
- An escarpment to walk under: `overrides: { from: { heartbeat: 'gentle-roughness', splatch: 'verdure-trio', landform: [{ op: 'scarp', path: [[-16,-5],[0,-9],[16,-8]], throw: 4.5, side: 'right' }, { op: 'strata', thickness: 0.6 }, { op: 'talus', scree: 0.12 }] } }`.
- An alpine range at 6 km: `overrides: { span: 6000, from: { heartbeat: 'rocky-irregular', splatch: 'glacier-trio', landform: [{ op: 'peaks', height: 9, center: [0,-12], radius: 20 }, { op: 'joints', rock: 'granite', spacing: 0.9 }, { op: 'talus' }], erosion: true } }`.
- A world you can orbit: add `planet: true` (or `{ radius: 30000 }`); with `horizon: 'sea'` the painting becomes a coast.
- A tower on the rim: add `place: [{ ref: '<a solid or building>', at: [0, -800], size: 30 }]`.
