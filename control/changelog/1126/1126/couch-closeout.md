### Couch closeout: the hill-climb caught up, and the seat values still missing

- The hill-climb registry now dates the mechanics the flight and metroidvania rails added without a date: a flight's
  `crash`, a lesson's `grade`, a rail stage's `wake` and the castle's `journey` (all 11-23). They now show in the page's
  "what was built lately". The side map's note now includes its boss room, its towers and atriums, and its page in the
  game book, and points to the metroidvania box for rooms on a grid. The in-game UI's note now includes the box's and
  the side map's HUD, minimap and pause on mana-ui, and the frame's hangar.
- What couch play still waits on is written down: versus beyond the fighter, free-for-all, teams, roles, split, window,
  turns and a lobby. The three couch probes blocked on them are party mash, driver and gunner, and split-screen
  deathmatch.
- The hill-climb prices couch play as one package (`couch`): versus, free-for-all, teams, roles, window, turns and the
  lobby. Each couch genre needs two or three of them at once, so priced one at a time none of them made a world. The
  lobby's old `seats` package is folded into it. Split screen stays its own package.
- A second, third or fourth player whose health runs out no longer lies down for the rest of the level. Their collapse
  plays, then it counts as a fall: a life is spent where lives are counted, and they get up beside the others with full
  health, or are out when none is left. The first player's knock-down still ends the level, as before.
- Each extra player's chip at the top of the screen shows a small bar of their own health, which follows it as they
  are hit. The first player's health stays on the level's own HUD.
- In a level that counts hits, every player now has their own health. Player 1 keeps the level's, and players 2–4 each
  get one shown in their own corner of the screen, in their colour. A player hit to nothing goes down, then a life is
  spent and they get up beside the others, or they are out.
- When the players count lives, player 1 is a player like the others: running out of health costs a life rather than
  ending the level, and the level is lost only when no player is left. Without lives, player 1's health running out
  ends the level, as before.
- Every player now works the level, not just the first. Any player picks up coins and items (into the team's count, once
  each, even when two reach one together) and can take the way out. A hazard hurts whoever steps in, in their own health.
- Players can now play against each other in a third-person deathmatch on one screen. With free-for-all or versus, each
  player is their own side: every player has their own health, frag count and corner of the screen, a fallen player
  comes back at a spawn point as the bots do, and the first to the frag limit wins the match, with the winner recorded
  for the results. In co-op the players are one side against the bots, and a player's hit on a partner scores nothing.
- In a team deathmatch, players split between red and blue: odd-numbered players on the first player's side, even on
  the other. A team deathmatch with a designed hero would not build at all before; it does now.
- The hill-climb marks versus, free-for-all and teams as built. The three couch genres still blocked now wait on the
  lobby, plus the time-window contest (party mash), roles (driver and gunner) or split screen (split deathmatch).
