### The space ground: a frame's gravity by name, and an asteroid in the belt

- A frame level says its gravity (`airspace.frame.gravity`): `planetary`, the frame's own and the default; `space`, the
  space ground (an asteroid, the Moon, a flat orbital platform) at a third of it, a game's and not a simulation's; or
  metres a second squared. Under a lighter gravity a frame's jump reaches the same height, slower, and a landing is
  hard at the speed that height falls to there (`assault-frame/pilot.js` `FRAME_GRAVITY`, `frameGravity`).
- A battlefield cube in the belt, `belt-asteroid` (a frame field's `asteroid` form): charcoal regolith struck by
  craters of every size, low ridges struck up between them, no skyline past a close horizon, a black sky with the stars
  out, the Sun a third of ours and a neighbouring rock hanging in it. Its standing rock is the massif's new `asteroid`
  card.
- An airless field is airless: no wind in its sound, its boost's dust the regolith's own colour, and the gen-7 sky
  over an airless cube (its heavens' void) holds no air, no cloud and no haze, only the stars and the Sun's disc. Every
  other page keeps its bytes.
