### Ask for a hooded mage: the character loop knows the wardrobe

- Asking for a character now reaches the ready outfits by name. The hero card the agent is routed to for "a character"
  names the fantasy six (`ranger`, `mage`, `peasant`, `noble`, `cleric`, `rogue`) beside the everyday cards, says that
  any outfit's cloak or robe takes a hood (worn up or down, a point, a depth), and that headgear takes a circlet and two
  hats (`circlet`, `travelerHat`, `wizardHat`). "A hooded mage", "a ranger in a cloak", "a noble with a circlet", "a
  cleric's robe" and "a wizard's hat" are phrasings the card answers (`routing-cards/hero.md`).
- Changing a character you already made reaches the same words: "put a hood on her", "give him a wizard's hat", "dress
  my hero as a rogue" (`routing-cards/revise-character.md`).
- The hero loop's dress and adorn steps teach them: pick a ready card, name each piece's cloth so it hangs and flows,
  set the hood up or down, add headgear, then read back the pieces the body did not wear and look at the cloth on a
  walk frame and the hood from the side (`catalysts/create-hero.md`). Detail and adornment still come last, each
  earning its place.
- The routing fixture carries the new phrasings, and a guard fails if the hero card stops naming a card of the fantasy
  wardrobe or names a headgear word that is not a card.
- Gates: machine (the routing card tests, the changed and critical tiers, both asks minted through the CLI). Eyes: not
  claimed.
