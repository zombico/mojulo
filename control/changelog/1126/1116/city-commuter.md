### Driving in a town and a city district

- **A hero can get into a parked car and drive.** In a game whose level is a town or a city district, the cars the
  town already parks are yours to take: walk up to one near where the level starts and press the interact key to get
  in. It is the same model in the same colour that was standing there. You drive it with the usual driving keys, and
  press again when it is nearly stopped to get out beside it and carry on on foot. Nothing has to be asked for.
- **A district with no car near its start has some at the kerb.** A city district keeps its cars in lots that can be
  blocks away, so where none stands within a short walk of the start, up to three cars of the city's own everyday
  fleet are parked at the kerb there.
- **A car stays on the street in a city district.** A driven car no longer climbs a building's raised ground, cars are
  parked only where the road is open from nose to tail, and a level that starts under an elevated highway now starts
  on the street below it rather than up on the highway.
- **Cars are solid to each other and to people.** A car you drive into a parked car stops against it and loses its
  speed instead of passing through, and you can no longer walk through a car on foot.
- **A city's traffic reacts to you.** The traffic already driving a city's main streets now brakes and queues behind
  your car, or behind you on foot, when you are in its lane, and drives on when you leave. Its vehicles are solid:
  drive into one and you stop against it. Cars parked for you at the kerb are kept out of the traffic's lanes.
- **Street paint lies on the road.** On a sloped city street the lane lines, streetcar rails and crosswalk bars used to
  sit level, each piece floating at one end and sunk at the other, so a hill read as a flight of steps. They now lie
  flat on the road's own slope, and every crosswalk sits on the road where it is rather than at its crossing's height.
- **Shops on a hill are entered on the level.** Where a row of walk-in shops in a city stands well above its sloping
  pavement, the pavement is now raised to the shops' floor along the whole front, so each door opens straight onto it
  instead of into a notch cut in a wall. The raised stretch meets the street at its uphill end, and at an end that
  stands above the street it has a ramp down where there is room, or a flight of steps beside it with a landing at
  the top. It is two metres wide, room for two people side by side, and railed wherever its edge stands over the
  street: along the front, across a closed end and down the steps. Rows more than three metres above their pavement
  are left as they were.
- **An errand can be driven: a fare.** In a town or city-district level with cars, an errand's pick-up and hand-in
  now stand well apart along the streets instead of a few steps from each other, and each is reached from the kerb:
  pull up beside it in a car and it counts, without getting out. A single-level world can now carry an errand on a
  town or a district, so a one-fare taxi run (pick up a passenger, drive, set down, get paid) is a game on its own.
