### An open city

- **A job that can be done again.** An errand can now be a standing job: its hand-in pays every time and no longer
  ends the level, and what it asks for can be picked up again straight after. A taxi fare, a delivery round or any
  repeatable job in a town or city district is one setting on the errand, and the city stays open around it.
- **Any parked car can be taken.** Every car a town or city district parks is now one you can get into and drive,
  wherever it stands, not only the few nearest the start. Vans and trucks from the city's fleets are included; a
  vehicle carrying something on its roof (a taxi's sign, a police light bar) is not yet.
- **A car can be taken out of traffic.** Walk up to a vehicle that is stopped or crawling in a city's traffic and the
  same prompt offers it; press and you are in it, and it is yours to drive. Up to six taken vehicles are kept at once;
  after that the one left standing farthest away is given up for the next.
- **People on the pavements.** A played city district now has pedestrians walking round the blocks near you on their
  pavements, some one way and some the other, following the ground up and down the hills. They are scenery for now:
  they do not react to you or to cars.
- **The people are made the way your hero is.** Each pedestrian is a person from the same character generator as the
  hero, rolled from the game's own seed in its setting, so they share its body, proportions, clothes and palette
  rather than being a separate stock figure. A handful are rolled for a district and shared among everyone walking,
  each at a slightly different height, and each is re-sampled to about a quarter of a played character's detail so a
  street of them stays light.
- **Cars sit on the road.** A car now pitches and leans with the ground under it instead of standing level on a
  slope, both parked and while you drive.
- **The city tells you what to do.** A town or city-district game with cars now opens with a line saying what it is,
  teaches the keys one at a time with your character in hand (walk, get in and out of a car, drive), and then keeps
  one instruction at the top of the screen with an arrow, the distance and a pillar of light where to go: get into
  a car, pick up the job, deliver it. A job that repeats sends you round again; with no job it says the city is open.
- **Your earnings stay on screen.** A repeating job's pay is shown as a running count in the corner, and the key hints
  now mention the car key.
- **Jobs send you across the district, to its own shops.** In a city-district game with cars, an errand's stops now
  stand at the district's shops rather than along the starting street, each chosen far from the start and from the
  others so a job is a drive. A stop you pull up to is at a shop's door; one bought over a counter is at the till
  inside a shop of that kind, so you park and walk in. The on-screen guide names the place ("Buy the gadget in the
  electronics shop", "Deliver the gadget to the gun store") and takes a job with a price in its right order.
- **One-off jobs and repeating jobs share a city.** Where a level has a repeating job, finishing a one-off job pays
  and the level carries on, so a district can hold a quest or two and a standing job at once.
- **Jobs are quests in a journal.** Every errand in a level is now also a quest: press J for the journal, which lists
  each job with its steps ticked as you do them, in the same words the on-screen guide uses. A line appears as a step
  is done and when a quest is complete.
- **The journal and its messages wear the game's look.** In a game with an interface look, the journal and the quest
  messages are drawn by the same interface system as the rest of the game's screens and HUD, instead of a separate
  plain panel. A game without one keeps the journal it had.
- **A map.** A played city now has a map. A small radar sits in the corner showing the streets and blocks around you
  with the way you face at the top, and M opens the full map: the whole place with its blocks, parks and water, your
  position, where to go next, each job's stops, the shops and the cars. V turns it between a straight-down plan, a
  tilted miniature with the buildings standing up, and a view that follows you; + and - zoom. A key beside it says
  what each icon means. The world pauses while it is open.
- **The map belongs to the game's interface, not to itself.** It is a new part of the game interface system, so it
  takes the game's theme like every other screen and has no colours of its own. What it shows is a setting on the
  game's interface: whether there is a radar and which way up, which views it turns through and which opens first,
  how far the radar sees, whether shops and cars are marked, and which icon stands for each kind of marker.
- **Three new icons**: a car, a shop and a parcel, in every icon style the themes offer.
