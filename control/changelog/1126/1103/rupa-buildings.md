### Destructible physics: whole buildings break, and stand at minute zero

A physical level (`destruction: 'physical'`) now breaks its big things by blocks too, not only its walls and props.

- **Towers and temples** (fractal structures): solid tiers, plinth steps and column shafts in courses no wider or taller
  than 3 m; hollow tiers and ring beams filled from their outlines with wall-thick cells that hold each other; a ring
  beam hung across its columns, seated on their capitals. Take a column and the beam over it sags or falls; take a
  course and what it held comes down.
- **A town's houses**: each is wall panels to its eave and a roof resting on them, breaking as the settlement they were
  built from: the style's facade word for the walls (adobe, hand-brick, limewash over rubble…), its roof word for the
  roof at a roof's weight. A settlement with no style of its own takes the cube's walls.
- **Towers and temples are stone**, as the cube paints them, unless the cube's `matter.structure` says otherwise.
- **Raised ground**: a building on a hill stands on its footing instead of dropping to the level's floor.
- **The load at minute zero**: what was built is checked by the page's own laws before anyone touches it (what falls at
  once, what is crushed, what sags), stamped on the world and never refused. Every structure preset stands.
- **Lighter pages**: blocks of one size share their pre-cut pieces; an arena's go from 1.7 MB to under 100 KB.
- **The load pass** finds supports through a grid, with the same answers, so a structure of hundreds of blocks stays quick.
- A level not physical changes no byte.
