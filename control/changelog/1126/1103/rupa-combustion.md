### Destructible physics: combustion that spreads by its heat, and the flame on what burns

A burning block now burns with a real flame, and its fire reaches what is near it the ways heat does.

- **The flame on what burns**: every block that can burn carries a flame from the flame library, sized by Heskestad's
  law from the heat its burning gives (a pine log's under a metre, an oak trunk's a couple of metres), kindling when the
  block catches, moving with it, leaning in the level's wind and dying back when it is doused or burnt out. Its smoke is
  destructible physics' own.
- **Fire spreads by its heat**: a burning block radiates a share of its heat from its flame to what faces it, by the
  solid angle it fills, so a crate catches across a gap; its plume heats what hangs over it, so a beam above a fire goes
  before one beside it; and it heats what it touches, as before.
