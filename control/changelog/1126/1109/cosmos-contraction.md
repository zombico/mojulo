### What to build next now counts cutscenes, game flow and acting

- **The planning map knows about the new story features.** The internal map the studio uses to decide what to build
  next now includes a game's whole order of play (its title, cutscenes, levels and ending), cutscenes shot by a
  director's rules, slow motion and freeze-frames inside scenes, and characters who act their lines. Games that need
  these now count as makeable, and the one missing piece next to them, holding the game world still while a cutscene
  plays, is priced and ranked so it can be weighed against other work.
- **The planning map knows about combos, beat-em-ups and horses.** It now tells a hero's single swing apart from strings
  of moves chained into combos (the weapon kits and their practice room), and counts horses a hero rides. Beat-em-ups
  down a street are listed as built but not yet reachable when designing a game, priced as one piece of work so they can
  be ranked against the rest.
- **Two new kinds of game to check against.** A stylish action game (combos, launchers, a gun) reads as makeable today;
  an open-world adventure on horseback waits on one seamless open land, which the map now describes more accurately: the
  world map's regions load as you walk into them, but by swapping the page at a gate, never streaming. The co-op street
  brawler now asks for the real beat-em-up street, so it shows the gap it actually has.
