### The metro's details: industrial windows, transformer cans, rooftop antennas, cell towers and blinking aviation lights

- **Industrial windows.** A works' hall is glazed in a ribbon high along its long walls. A warehouse is glazed along its
  back and its ends, and a works' office, a substation's control house and a storage yard's gatehouse each have a row
  of windows a storey on every side. The glazing is drawn proud of the wall with the glass's sheen. Past dusk some of it
  is lit (a pane over the glass, so a night metro is still its day's faces plus its lit panes).
- **Transformer cans.** Every fourth wooden pole along an outskirts or industrial street carries a pole-mounted
  transformer (`kit.js` `can`): its drum and lid below the crossarm, hung off the side away from its lamp, with a
  bracket, two bushings and a drop wire up to the crossarm. Like the street's other tall kit, it is taken out from
  under a highway's deck.
- **Rooftops** (`geocity/rooftop.js`). Each flat roof of a low or mid-rise building (mid-rises, slabs, podiums,
  walk-ups, low-rises, shops, schools, control houses, 7 to 75 m tall) is dressed by its own dice: air handlers on their
  curbs, a cell site's three sectors of panel antennas and their cabinet, a microwave dish at an edge facing out, a mast
  with its yagi booms, and on the lower ones a satellite dish, all turned one way. They are drawn near the camera.
- **Cell towers.** A 30 m monopole with its triangular head and three sectors stands in a corner of some car parks and at
  the back of some works and storage yards. It is seen across its district.
- **Aviation lights** (a new page channel, `scene/channels/beacons.js`, the payload's `beacons`). Red lights blink
  together on the world clock (1.6 s, on a third of it), so a capture at a clock and the live page agree. They sit at
  two opposite corners of the roof of every tower over 120 m, the other two half way up one over 220 m, and on top of
  every cell tower. Each is a round soft dot with a faint glow that keeps its size on the screen however far off it
  is. Without beacons the page adds not one byte.
