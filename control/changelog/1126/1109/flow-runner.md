### A game's flow, from boot to credits, as one thing a game can say

- **A game can say its whole flow.** What plays when, from the first screen to the credits, is now something a game
  can describe in one place: its screens, its cinematics, its levels and short holds, and where each one leads. The
  checker explains every mistake: a step nothing reaches, a step with no way out, a level the game does not have, or
  cinematics that would loop forever with nothing for the player to press. A game that says nothing gets the flow it
  has always had, worked out from its title screen, its levels, its runs and its ending.
- **Games now run on their flow.** Every game that uses the light shell (every game the studio designs) now moves
  between its screens and levels by following its flow, so a game that describes its own order plays in that order:
  a beat between screens, a level that goes straight on to the next, a title that moves on by itself when left alone.
  A game that describes nothing plays exactly as before. Two small fixes come with it: the loading veil now lifts once
  the level has drawn its first picture rather than while it is still building, and after a level ends the game
  moves on as soon as its end card has been read, at once when the level shows no card, or early when the player
  presses a key.
- **Cinematics between screens.** A game's flow can now play a cutscene on any of its levels as a step of its own:
  an opening before the title, or a prologue after New Game that hands straight over to play on the same level with
  no reload. A cutscene can be made unskippable. If the level has no such scene, the game simply moves on.
- **Scenes no longer get lost.** When two cutscenes are triggered at once, or one is triggered while another plays,
  they now play one after another instead of the later one being dropped. A scene that should open a level now waits
  until the game has handed the level its saved progress, so it can no longer start too early.
- **A game's story can have an opening, a prologue and an ending.** A moment in a game's story can now be its
  opening, its prologue or its ending, staged on one of its levels. The opening plays before the title on the first
  launch and again when the title is left alone, like an attract loop. The prologue plays after New Game and, on the
  first level, hands over to play without a reload. The ending plays after the game is first completed, before the end
  card. A game whose story has none of these is unchanged.
- **The flow is a page in the studio.** When a game's designer wants a different order, they can now write the
  game's flow for the whole game, naming its levels as the world names them. The review shows the flow as a
  storyboard: each step, which part of the studio it comes from, what it waits for and where it leads. If the main
  quest's ending is played as a story moment, that moment now becomes the game's ending cutscene, unless the story
  has an ending of its own. The studio's guidance makes the flow the main agent's own page, never handed off.
