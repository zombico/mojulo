### Wider fair-weather cumulus, not popcorn

The flight sky's cumulus read as popcorn: heaped clusters of small round puffs, about as tall as they were wide. Seen
from under them, their bellies stacked into horizontal slabs. They now read as fair-weather cumulus, wide and flat-based
with a gentle dome, across every game under the flight sky (Wolf Squadron's ground stages, Pilot School, any airspace
level).

- **Wider and shallower** (`skyscape/sky.js`): fair weather's are 900–2400 m across and 220–560 m deep, where they were
  450–1300 and 350–800, so there are fewer, broader clouds for the same cover. A hazy day's small cumulus are widened
  alike.
- **One body, not a heap** (`scene/channels/flight-sky.js`):
  - most of each cloud's puffs are low over its base, spread across its whole footprint;
  - each cloud is drawn out a third longer along the wind;
  - its puffs are larger and overlapping, each a little wider than tall;
  - its rims are softer;
  - its light comes mostly from its height (bright up its tops, shaded under its belly), so puffs never read as balls.
- **No slabs under them.** Each puff is a camera-facing card, and the flat base cut each card where it crossed the base
  height. Seen from below, the tilted cards met the base at different heights on screen and stacked into slabs. A fair
  cloud's low puffs now sit higher over the base and fade into it over 45 m, and the belly's shade is the cloud's own
  depth, not each puff's.
- A storm's towering cumulonimbus keeps its shape: its puffs stay round, its foot cut flat.
