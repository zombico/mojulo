### The metroidvania box heard

- Vampire Sonata and XenoJager are on the sound sheet with sounds of their own, where a box level heard only its HUD
  before.
  - **The castle.** Erina's longsword cuts the air, cuts into the dead and rings off plate, and her boots strike
    flagstones. A foe felled is unmade, and a boss falls as a great body. Its menus play the crypt's set, as its HUD does,
    no longer the rail's robot.
  - **The planet.** A sealed suit's arm cannon, its servos and boots, its crystal rock; its menus and its HUD on the
    terminal's set.
  - A game's own `sound.flavor` names its recordings (a box variant no longer borrows its rail's robot), and
    `sound.menus` names its menus' set.
- Every boss on the director is heard.
  - Each move's wind-up plays as it is told, and its strike as it lands.
  - Its voice is read from what it is. It roars at its first move and as its phases turn, is hurt when struck, and dies
    as its last form falls.
  - A second form rises with a surge.
  - A game's `sound.bosses` overrides any of these words.
- A metroidvania's held gates are heard. The boss's room is barred by a portcullis and winched open after the fight. A
  cracked wall crumbles, a secret wall falls away into its hollow, a grate scrapes aside, a lock turns and a boss's seal
  shatters.
- Foes drawn as the undead, the orcish or the infernal are heard as their own voices (the dead, an ogre, a demon), never
  as people's shouts. A wolf that braces to charge braces in its own threat, not a motor's rev. The ogre and the demon
  are new voice classes.
- A sheet word the shelf has but nothing voices here (a sound only the library records, where it is not installed) is
  silent rather than an error.
- The sound library records the boss kit, the two new voices, and the castle's and the planet's own sounds.
