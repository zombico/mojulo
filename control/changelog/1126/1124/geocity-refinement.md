### A city at street level

- A town's or a district's level can now ask for its sky and air in its style, the same way it asks for its hour:
  clouds, haze or mist lying over the streets, the far blocks softened in it, and a finish (one tone curve, the sky
  reflected in glass, light that blooms). It can also ask for the view cone, which draws the street's kit and trees
  only as far as they read. Hijack City asks for all of them.
- The camera behind a vehicle you drive is framed by its size: a car is seen as before, a van, a box truck or a rig
  from further back and higher, so the road ahead shows over its roof; and the camera comes in front of a wall rather
  than standing inside the building behind you. A vehicle taken from the traffic is framed by its own size too.
- Suburbs can be a West Coast tract or a tropical coast, not only a northeastern streetcar suburb or a European old
  town. A West Coast street is one-storey stucco ranches and Spanish revival bungalows on wide lots, each with its own
  drive and garage, palms along a grass parkway, pools out back, and stucco shops behind their parking on the boulevard.
  A tropical street is pastel block houses raised a step under white tile, louvred shutters, low walls and iron gates,
  and coconut palms. A city's outskirts carry the same look out to its edge, with its houses, shops, flats and street
  trees all in it, while its downtown stays its own.
- Their shopping streets are a Rodeo: two-storey boutiques of cream and white stucco over limestone, tall display
  glass in black frames, crisp canopies and gold lettering, under palms. The tropical coast's are the same in pastel.
  The strip malls stay on the boulevards.
- San Francisco, in two neighbourhoods. A Victorian one: narrow wooden houses touching up steep streets, each painted
  its own three colours (Italianates and Stick houses under flat false fronts and cornices, Queen Annes under gables,
  bays up their fronts, stoops to their doors, Edwardian flats among them, corner stores). And the Avenues: rows of
  two-storey pastel stucco houses each over its own garage, a picture window above it, a car on every apron, few
  trees. A hill city to set them in, on a peninsula with water on three sides and cable cars up its streets.
- Hijack City by locale: Los Angeles (ranches and palms in a low gold sun and a warm smog), Miami (pastel houses
  and coconut palms under a high noon sun, a bright sea haze, vivid colour), San Francisco (the hill city in its
  morning fog under a grey sky, cool), Boston (a brick inner city on an overcast afternoon, filmic) and Toronto (its
  streetcar suburbs on a fair morning). Each is one word that sets the suburbs, the part of town, the light, the sky,
  the air and the colour grade together; any of them can still be named over it.
- Each locale builds its city its own way, from the old metro city's flavours: Los Angeles low and wide in cream and
  sand stucco with few towers on a nearly flat basin; Miami's slender white and glass condo towers with stacked
  balconies on flat land by the water; San Francisco's pale stone and glass on its steep hills; Boston's brick and
  brownstone without New York's fire escapes and water tanks, on low hills.
- Hijack City's suburbs are the West Coast's now; one word makes them a tropical coast, the northeastern suburb they
  were, or a San Francisco district in its fog.
- No building floats off its ground any more: where the land falls away under a wall (a slope, a terrace, a
  waterfront ramp), that wall is carried down to the ground on a plinth, and a door level with its plaza keeps its
  threshold. The hill city's land eases down to its water instead of standing on a cliff over it.
- The city's glass gives back the sky: windows, shop fronts and glass towers mirror it by angle and catch the sun's
  highlight, where before they were painted flat and dark. Parked cars wear a clear coat; the traffic and the car you
  drive gleam the same way, the sky in their roofs and doors and the sun glinting off their panels.
- Roads, walks and plazas keep their own colour under a low sun instead of turning navy, and a highway's underside is
  textured concrete instead of a dark flat slab.
- Highways no longer run into buildings: every elevated highway now has its own corridor, the deck plus 3 m of
  clearance each side, and the arterial under it is laid that wide before any block is cut, so nothing is built under
  or against a deck (a wider sidewalk runs under it instead).
- The city has places now: some neighbourhoods have a church, a mosque or a temple on its forecourt and lawn behind a
  railing; some have a cemetery with rows of headstones, crosses and ledgers, gravel walks, trees and a chapel at the
  gate. Which faiths a city has is a setting (`civic.faiths`), and `civic: false` turns them off.
- Big city parks are no longer bare lawn: promenades cross at an obelisk, each quarter has a playground, a pavilion,
  benches and trees, and the park is railed along its streets. Park blocks are railed too, and car parks are lined with
  bollards along their streets.
- Places of worship are part of the city downtown too: in the core and the inner city a church, mosque or temple is
  built into a block on a street corner among its neighbours, or fills a small downtown block of its own on a paved
  plaza, instead of standing on a lawn behind a railing (that churchyard form stays for the suburbs).
- Downtown streets are dressed: dashed centre lines, zebra crossings at every junction, cars parked along both kerbs
  with parking meters, trees in their pits between the lamps, hydrants and newspaper boxes at the corners.
- Hijack City's downtown is the city's core now: its towers, podiums and square, with the inner city round it (the
  suburbs are their own district).
- A side street no longer dead-ends into the main road's sidewalk: where it meets an arterial the sidewalk is opened
  across its mouth and the road runs through, and no bench, lamp, bin or tree stands in the way.
- Downtown shops hang blade signs over the sidewalk, and the roofs are dressed: towers carry a mechanical penthouse
  and cooling towers, flat roofs a stair bulkhead, a row of air handlers and, on the big ones, a cooling tower.
- The city's ground level looks lived in: some windows have blinds drawn or curtains, some homes have a window air
  conditioner, a few shops are shut behind roller shutters (some tagged with graffiti), and open shops put things out
  on the sidewalk: sandwich boards, planters by the door, a cafe table under an umbrella, crates, bags out for
  collection. Downtown streets get manhole covers, oil stains where cars park, utility cabinets, mailboxes and the odd
  vendor's cart. Each is placed by its own position, so nothing else in the city moves.
- Buildings no longer share one wall colour: each building's walls are a little lighter or darker and warmer or
  cooler than its neighbours', and some wear brick where their neighbours are stone (or the other way round).
- About a third of open shops show a lit interior through their glass (a shelf, a counter, someone behind it), lit by
  its own lamps, so it glows the same at noon and at night.
- Roadworks: some streets and main roads have a crew's works pickup or van (or a delivery truck) standing in the kerb
  lane, a barrier behind it and cones tapered round it.
- Alleys: more than half of the inner city's blocks are now split down the middle by a service alley running street to
  street, with the backs of buildings on both sides (windows, balconies, no shopfronts), dumpsters and bags along the
  walls, oil stains, a manhole, wires strung across overhead and sometimes a car or a delivery van. The sidewalks open
  at each alley's mouth, and nothing is parked or stood across it. `alleys: false` turns them off.
- Night scapes: Hijack City can be played at night (`hour: 'night'` or `'dusk'`), each locale with its own night (LA's
  sodium-amber lamps in a lit smog, Miami's white lamps, San Francisco's fog, Boston's cold white, Toronto's warm). The
  city falls dark so what is lit stands out: windows, lit shop interiors, a pool of light under every street lamp and
  alley lamp with a glow at its head, and the traffic's headlights and tail lights. Alleys now have wall lamps.
- Pedestrians, in sixes: the played district's sidewalks are walked by groups of six different people (two pairs side
  by side and two on their own, each at its own pace and some going the other way round). Busier blocks (a square,
  towers, a main street) get more sixes than quiet residential ones, filled nearest the middle first. A level's
  `crowd` sets how heavy it is: `quiet`, `busy` or `packed` (one, two or three rolls of six different people, up to
  four, eight or twelve sixes walking); downtown and the inner city are busy and the suburbs quiet by default.
- Fixed: a hero starting downtown could find no car to take at the kerb, since a downtown parked car nearby kept the
  kerb cars from being laid; the kerb cars are laid now, never on a bay already taken.
- Realistic heroes' hands fit their bodies: a hand is now as long as an adult's for the body's height (about a ninth of
  it) and as broad across the knuckles as a man's or a woman's, where it had been a fifth short and narrow. Heroes made
  before keep their hands; a new one, or one remade, gets the new size.
- Athleisure outfits, drawn for women (any outfit dresses anyone): `yoga` (a matching long-sleeved crop and leggings),
  `jogger` (a running tank and brief shorts in runners), `bikeShorts` (an oversized tee over bike shorts), `tracksuit`
  (a zipped track jacket with ribbed cuffs and waist over a tee, tapered joggers), `studio` (a zip-up left open over a
  crop and leggings), `tennis` (a collared knit top and a crisp short skirt) and `lounge` (a loose cropped sweatshirt and
  wide sweatpants). Street people wear them now and then.
- Realistic faces look more like their own people (new heroes; existing ones are unchanged):
  - Eye colour now comes from ancestry: mostly brown, with blue, green, grey and hazel mainly for European ancestry.
    Every face used to have the same blue-grey eyes.
  - A hero in clothes keeps their own hair, brow and lash colour. Dressing used to turn everyone's hair the same dark
    brown.
  - Women's faces read as women's: larger eyes, a smaller and finer nose, a narrower jaw, a softer, smaller
    chin, a flatter brow ridge and fuller lips. Bold features (a square jaw, a strong chin, heavy brows) are rarer, and a
    woman's overall character leans soft and delicate while still reaching the full range.
- Street people mix ancestries (no more than two of one in a six) and their faces vary more than a hero's do.
- Realistic women's bodies have a flat belly and a navel: the six-pack is now a smooth toned front, the pouch under the
  navel and the mounds at the front of the hips are gone, and the navel shows (it was too small for the body's mesh to
  carry). Heavier builds keep their belly. New heroes only; existing ones keep their bodies.
- Realistic people's heads and necks are their own: head size varies a little person to person (it was one size for
  every woman and every man of a height), neck length varies, and the neck's width follows the head on it. Heavyset
  necks are thick but no longer wider than the head. New heroes only; existing ones keep their bodies.
- Hijack City can be walked by realistic people (`people: 'realistic'`, opt-in): the street's cast of realistic men and
  women in everyday clothes, their ancestries drawn from the city's own mix. Each person is drawn in full detail near the
  camera (their face kept) and simplified further away, and a level's people are baked once and kept on disk, so a
  level opens quickly after the first time.
- Realistic pedestrians are lit by the city: shaded by the sun by day, brighter under street lamps and dimmer between
  them by night (they used to glow at night and look flat by day).
- Pedestrians in a group no longer walk inside each other: partners walk side by side and the groups along a block
  are spread out. Before, each pair was drawn on one spot, so two people's clothes showed through each other in
  patches.
- Hijack City builds again: since its district moved downtown, the contact's jobs had no shops to stand at and the level
  could not be laid. Downtown's shopfronts are now the jobs' stops (the kerb before each shop).
- A big level's loading screen no longer disappears before the level is ready. Exported games' levels now say how their
  download is going (the percentage and what is loading: the world, the people, then building the world) and the
  loading screen stays up until the first frame is drawn. Before, it was taken down after 45 seconds, leaving a blank
  screen while a large level was still loading. The level's files also download at the same time instead of one after
  another.
- The loading screen also covers a level's warm-up: it stays up, saying "warming up", until the game runs smoothly,
  instead of lifting on the first frame and leaving the game frozen for a while. Realistic pedestrians' detailed
  figures are built during that warm-up (one body shared by everyone wearing it), so walking up to someone no longer
  hitches the game.
