### Going up in Galaxy Boy and Jumping Giuseppe: climbs built, storeys thick, terraces down

- Galaxy Boy's side map takes the castle's climbs as two new climb forms (`docs/side-map-principles.md` "Going up is
  earned"): a `tower`, a screen wide, and an `atrium`, two screens wide round a core. Each is climbed by its structure
  (stairs walked across it, landings on its walls and the core, a ladder at each turn), with the way woven through the
  core from screen to screen and the frame following it both ways. Every piece is solid. The lowest flight is built up
  from the floor and turned to the way in, or a ladder stands where it would wall the way off.
- A climb of more than a screen is a building's: a storey's floor spans it at every screen of height, as thick as a
  building's (`SLAB`), open where the way rises through it. Those openings are its chutes, seen down through and
  dropped down. A long climb keeps its middle floors instead of hanging as one run of stairs in a void.
- Galaxy Boy's first level climbs an atrium through the plant's heart after its gauntlet, before the chute down to the
  boss. The side map's bot climbs towers and atriums, stairs walked, leaps leapt and ladders held. The side map's
  `reach` law reads a structure's stands, and its ladders step off where their landing is.
- Jumping Giuseppe's strip: a face climbed by kicks is a tower of the orchard, with quoins down its ends, string courses
  and round windows under its cornice. A segment that ends high comes back down across terraces, each a share of the
  jump down and a landing long, so going down is going across and each can be climbed back up, never a cliff dropped
  off.
- The castle's planner learned from both: a ladder whose foot landing would stand low is taken on down to the floor; a
  lowest flight that would wall the way in is a ladder instead; an exit at a wall's top or a doorway is never a ladder's
  head; and the turns' ladders, the flights' goings and the exit's gallery are tried in turn until the turns fall clear
  of the floor's ways. A room's second climb is a zigzag round its structure, clear of it.
