### Going up in Galaxy Boy and Jumping Giuseppe: storeys and chutes, towers and terraces

- Galaxy Boy's side map has two new climb forms for long climbs, both climbed as a wall-climbing hero climbs (never by
  stairs): a `tower`, a screen wide, and an `atrium`, two screens wide (`docs/side-map-principles.md` "Going up is
  earned"). Each is built in storeys, with a thick floor at every screen of height (`SLAB`), so a long climb keeps its
  middle floors.
- Each storey is risen through a chute in the floor over it: a chimney of the shaft's wall and an inner wall, its rests
  within three kicks, entered under the inner wall and left over it, the last over the far wall's top. The chutes
  stand at the two ends in turn, so every storey is crossed between kicks. A ladder stands only where a wall kick has
  nothing to kick off: an atrium's middle storey is risen by a ladder through a chute in the open (and a hero with no
  wall kick climbs every storey by ladders).
- Galaxy Boy's first level climbs an atrium through the plant's heart after its gauntlet, before the chute down to the
  boss. The side map's bot climbs towers and atriums storey by storey, and the `rests` law holds their chimneys to three
  kicks. A ladder steps off where its floor is.
- Jumping Giuseppe's strip: a face climbed by kicks is a tower of the orchard, with quoins down its ends, string courses
  and round windows under its cornice. A segment that ends high comes back down across terraces, each a share of the
  jump down and a landing long, so going down is going across and each can be climbed back up, never a cliff dropped
  off.
- The castle's climb planner learned to handle more rooms: a ladder whose foot landing would stand low is taken on down
  to the floor; a lowest flight that would wall off the way in is a ladder instead; an exit at a wall's top or a
  doorway is never a ladder's head; and the turns' ladders, the flights' goings and the exit's gallery are tried in turn
  until the turns fall clear of the floor's ways. A room's second climb is a zigzag round its structure, clear of it.
