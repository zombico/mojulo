### The metroidvania box on its geocube

- A geocube can say a metroidvania box's look: its `box` names a library (the castle, XenoJager's planet), and the
  cube supplies the rest. Its colours tint each ring toward the cube's ramps, keeping the library's lightness so the
  light still pools and the walk still reads. Its crystals give the gem and its glow, and `rock` gives the boulders.
- A box level whose words name no theme now takes its look from its world's geocube. `gothic-night` builds the castle,
  and XenoJager's cube (`XENOJAGER_CUBE`, a document over gothic-night) builds the planet. A cube without a box keeps the
  greybox, and a box whose words name a theme is drawn exactly as before.
- The planet's crystals are drawn in centimetre paths, as the land's are, so a sapphire reads blue rather than black.
- The game book has a page for a box level, read off the box as its level builds it:
  - the plan: rooms in their ring's colour, the closed parts, ledges and ladders, the doorways and holes, each gate in
    its colour, pockets, saves, bosses, keys, the powers and the way in;
  - a card for each ring: its look, locales, what it is built of, its hazard, light, climbs, what it holds and the gate
    on inward;
  - the powers and what each opens;
  - every law the box is held to, labelled as the machine gate.
- The game book opens for a metroidvania level and a side map level. Both used to stop the book before its first page.
