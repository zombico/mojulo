### Flight games heard

- A flown level hears its craft. How it is flown picks the kit: a light plane on the fly rule is a trainer, a fighter
  down a rail an arcade fighter, and a starfighter flown from its seat a cockpit. Pilot School, Wolf Squadron and Rebel
  Storm sound with nothing written, where every flight page was silent before.
  - The trainer has its prop engine by the throttle, the air over it and its wheels on the runway by its speed, the
    stall warning while it stalls, a touchdown soft or hard, a crash and a ring flown through.
  - The arcade fighter has its engine from cruise to boost, its lasers and the foes', its boost, brake and barrel roll, a
    shield hit, and fighters, ground guns and the gunship boss blown apart.
  - The cockpit has its engines by the throttle, its laser cannon and the foes', shield and hull hits, the targeting
    computer seeking and locking, a torpedo away, the incoming warning, flares, a fighter roaring past, a fighter and a
    warship blown apart, and a scrape along a hull.
- Sounds are heard by their distance out to a flight's kilometres, not an arena's 900 m.
- A level's own audio keeps its cues over the kit's. `flightSound` changes or silences one event, and `audio: false`
  silences the level.
- The sound library records the kits (`cards/flight.js`): the starfighters' engines at steady throttles, crossfaded as a
  car's are, and every new sound, each heard as a stand-in where the library is not installed.
