### Northeast suburbs

- **In progress.** A Northeastern US suburb for towns and cities: colonials and Cape Cods on wide lawns with their
  driveways, garden apartments round their courts, towers in the park over their plazas, strip shops on the arterials.
- A town can be a Northeast suburb (`pattern: 'northeast'`): colonials in white, yellow or sage clapboard and red brick,
  Cape Cods under their steep roofs, each detached on a wide lot behind a short lawn with its own driveway and garage,
  no cars on the street; and garden apartments, long brick blocks of flats on their lawns along the avenue.
- Clapboard: painted lap siding, each board lapping over the one below, for any style's walls.
- A city's inner ring can be low-rise: brick walk-ups, four-storey low-rises and main streets of shops among its
  midrises, and its outskirts can hold garden apartments round their shared gardens. A new Northeast metro (a Boston)
  is built that way.
- Hijack City can be played in the inner city or the suburbs, not only downtown: a district is chosen by a word, and any
  city's district can be asked for by its ring.
- A city's street trees stand on the walk, behind the kerb and clear of every crossing, in a game or out of one; none
  were left standing in the road where it is shown rather than played.
- Sidewalk trees are small street trees (flowering cherries, birches, maples of 6 to 10 m), each in a box of soil in
  the walk; an outskirts walk is wider to hold them with a clear way past. Park, yard and garden trees keep their size.
- The outskirts' buildings stand closer to their walks: houses a short front yard back, low-rises and walk-ups on their
  street with their lots behind (no longer in the middle of their blocks), garden apartments a few metres back; less
  grass between the walk and the wall.
- Apartment complexes (low-rises, walk-ups, garden apartments) are fenced along their streets with a low black iron
  fence, a paved path from each front door across the lawn to a gate in it.
