### Shops you can walk into

- **A town's shops can be entered.** Ask for it on a town (its `enter` setting) and each shop on a main street gets a real
  doorway and a furnished interior behind it, taken from the same store designs the shopping mall uses: clothing,
  electronics, a bookshop, homewares (a cafe or a food hall when you name them). A town made without it is unchanged.
- **Interiors are drawn only when you are close.** Each inside appears as you approach its door and is left undrawn
  from further away, so a street of shops costs little until you walk it.
- **You can see into an open shop from the street.** Its windows are now real glass: the shelves and racks inside show
  through from the pavement, and the street shows through from inside.
- **A city's shop rows can be entered too.** The same setting on a city opens every shop along its main streets and
  neighbourhood edges, and any town built into that city follows. Shops of the same size share one interior, so a
  city of dozens of shops carries only a handful of them. In a city the shop windows are still painted, not glass.
- **Five new kinds of shop for a street.** A convenience store, a pharmacy, a diner, a pawn shop and a gun store join
  the store designs, and each can also be made on its own or placed in a shopping mall.
- **A street decides who rents its shops.** A main street leans to clothing, books and homewares, a busy avenue to
  electronics, convenience stores and pharmacies, an old town to bookshops and clothing. No two neighbours are the same
  kind, and each shop's sign takes its store's colour. You can still name the kinds yourself.
- **A city shop has a real door and a step up to it.** Its doorway now clears a person's height, and where the shop
  stands above the pavement a short flight of steps leads up to it. A shop that would need too long a flight stays shut.
