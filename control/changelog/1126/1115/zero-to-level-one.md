### From an idea to a first level: how a game is started

- **A new visitor is offered four ways to start.** When someone opens the studio with nothing in mind yet, the agent
  now offers to make a character, design a game, design an object or make a 3D exhibit. Someone who arrives knowing
  what they want is taken straight there.
- **A game starts from what you bring.** Choose a game and the agent asks whether you have drawings, plans or
  documents to turn into the game's plan. It uses what they say, sets aside what it cannot place, and asks only about
  what is still missing.
- **The first session aims at a first level you can play.** The studio's guidance to the agent now says plainly that
  the work of a first session runs from the idea to its first level: who is in it, what the player does again and
  again, the level built, how it feels, and a game that loads it and leads to the next one. Most games are made of
  levels played this way; an open world is made differently, and the guidance says so.
- Designed, not built yet: one step that reads your material and reports which of the game's first decisions it
  already answers.
