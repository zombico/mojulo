### What to build next now counts a world's people, its colour and how a game starts

- **A second pass over the same merges, for what a game can now ask for.** The first pass over the work merged since
  November 10th dated a few things and left the rest in its notes, as plumbing the planning map does not count. This
  one counts the parts of that work a game can ask for, each with the day it was built: a game that starts in play
  behind an opening cutscene, a level session that asks its set-up first (difficulty, picks) and then loads, plays,
  pauses and shows its results, a world map that opens by teaching the controls and then sending foes at the hero, a
  fight that comes when the one before it is won, an obstacle course run at speed that is different every play,
  goblins and orcs as peoples of their own, a people's foes left wild, each geared by its own role, or dressed alike
  as one band, the cast coloured so it stands clear of the land it walks on, two ways a game's land can be coloured (a
  quiet land that leaves colour to the cast, a day that turns to night across the levels), a setting's history kept
  on the game, and a hero designed as a person of its world.
- **Six things that are made but cannot yet be asked for in a designed game are priced**, so they can be ranked
  against other work: a power system from a setting's lore (what opposes what, a reserve of power, who holds a share,
  a monster manual, a skill tree), a room's state of repair in one word (kept, worn, neglected, derelict, ruined),
  books a player finds and reads in a level, one composed room as a level of a game, a finished frame (bloom, the sky
  in shiny surfaces), and a story moment filmed by naming it. The first of these is now named for what it would be:
  orders from lore.
