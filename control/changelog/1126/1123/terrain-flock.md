### Trees as flock

- Realistic trees' crowns are drawn as a model maker's clump foliage is cut: a porous sponge the sky shows through and
  the sun lights from behind, its edges breaking into the tree's own leaves at their real size (an oak's lobed leaf,
  a beech's oval one). It is the realistic crown's look from now on; a scene can turn it off, leave the leaves off, or
  choose its shadow: dappled, solid or none. Far trees are the same sponge, so a tree keeps its look as it
  recedes; exported models keep the crown as it was.
- Leafy bushes on the same scenes are flock too: a box or laurel dome stands as clumps of sponge, and a forsythia's
  fountain as runs of sponge along its arches, each with its own small leaves on the skin and the light through it, near
  and far. Hostas, ferns and the needled
  bushes keep their own look.
- A spike compares the crown as it was with the sponge and the sponge with leaves from the same cameras
  (`scripts/spikes/flock`).
- In the fall, the trees shed as they turn: each its own way, its crown opening onto its limbs from the top and the
  outside in, its last leaves catching the sun, more light reaching the ground, a wood never one density.
- Geotowns and geocities take the same trees: their street trees and their gardens' bushes read as realistic flock
  when the scene asks for realistic crowns. Street trees grow away from the houses, over the street, so a town keeps its
  trees; and a residential street is a mix of maples, flowering cherries and birches at a street's sizes, not a forest.
