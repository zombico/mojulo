### The geocube's exaggerate: a town drawn by a hand that leans

- **`exaggerate` on the geocube** (`era/exaggerate.js`), a style word beside `register`: how the art director bends the
  shape of what the cube's builders build, as the register says how it is inked. A name (`storybook`, `crooked`,
  `street`), `{ from, …changes }`, or the terms: `lean` (degrees off plumb at a foot), `sway` (metres between the turns
  of the lean), `sag` (eaves and ridges out of level), `reach` (the height past which the lean stops growing: a cottage
  slumps, a tower sways), `elements` (the language caricatured), `kit` (`chunk`, `lean`: the street's furniture) and
  `seed`. Absent, every byte as it was.
- **The plan is law, the shape is free.** The bend is one seeded field over the world, zero at each building's foot and
  growing with height, so streets, lots, pads, colliders, anchors, doors and routes are untouched by construction, and a
  wall two buildings share moves once (a terrace never cracks). Laid after the tiles, so the courses lean with their
  wall; the faces re-lit on their bent normals. Its laws advise: `foot` (what a door's head moves), `fold` (under 1 the
  bend can open no crack and push no two buildings together: a gap g stays at least (1 − fold)·g) and `plumb`.
- **The geocity bent**: its masses, night panes and an evocation's facades on their feet (a construction frame stays
  plumb under its scaffold). **The street's furniture exaggerated** hardest (small things exaggerate most): the geocity
  kit's lamps, signals, poles, bins and hydrants chunked, its lamps and signals leant over their reach; a geotown's
  lamps, signals, signs and hydrants chunked and leant. A geotown's houses stay as they were.
- **A loud shopfront** (`exaggerate.front`, `geotown/front-loud.js`; the `street` hand carries one): a shop's front as a
  toy street draws it, the same shop pushed. Fat frames and mullions proud of glass set deep, a kick plate in its own
  ink, the door in fat jambs; the sign too big and a little askew in a dark rim, its letters blocks that read against
  it; the awning striped with a scalloped valance; posters slapped on the glass; ink splashed across the front; every
  colour pushed toward saturation. Each shop is its OWN: its walls leant to a hue of their own and a false front of its
  own form (stepped, an arch, a gable, battlements), neither its row neighbour's. Two principles hold on it: **no
  window square** (`skew`: every light's corners drawn inward, at least three off 90°, its frame, bars, sill and lintel
  warped with it, the shop's glazing and its storeys' lights alike; `speak`'s `skew`) and **no two connected buildings
  one height** (`step`: the false front raised until it stands that far from its neighbour; the `rooflines` law). The
  door and the plan stay where they were.
  Along a main street's ROW the shops' storeys are each its own too: its lights' surrounds, sills and lintels and its
  cornice in its own ink, never its neighbour's (the row's cornice no longer one band across it); in the toon register
  each shop's front by its own dice (its sign, its members, its tilt, its stripes, its posters), no two agreeing. Its
  service yard holds the study's receptacles (a front-load dumpster where it has the room, else wheelie bins: true to
  size, askew in a toon register) and its car is a toy car.
  An EDGE's avenue row (shops, offices, a converted house) is built attached and even: its offices and its converted
  houses each raise a false front of their own, so no two stand one height (a crown is chosen from the building's real
  top, its roof's ridge or a sign above its wall included); its LANE is laid in a muted patchwork of skewed slabs and
  splashed with ink, each garage on it its own hue never the next one's, its door an ink, and a wheelie bin of the
  study out by each garage's door.
- **A loud metro** (the geocity, by the same `homes` and `streets`): each mass (a house pair, a shop, a low-rise, a
  walk-up, a tower) its own wall hue and roof ink, never the last one's; along a row of houses or shops no two
  neighbours one height (the next raised by the step where the dice stood it level); its lane lines fat and its yellow
  pushed, its crosswalk bars parallelograms, ink splashed along its arterials; an evoked block's walks in skewed pastel
  slabs (the metro's others too far off to see them); its parked cars toy cars. Its composed geotowns are loud by
  their own. Its street furniture (bins, hydrants, benches, bollards, bike rings, newspaper boxes) each its own ink,
  never the last of its kind's: its templates drawn pale, each stamp its tint. Its evoked facades each lot a hue of its
  own, never the lot before it, the facade's faces and the card's painted grid leant to it, their light kept. Their
  windows are geometry (`geocity/facade-loud.js`, under `homes.skew`): each wall's window card made a wall sheet and,
  per window, its glass and a fat frame proud of it in its lot's ink (never the lot before it's), every window off
  square at three corners or more (a `windows` law), a night's lit rooms burning in the windows their panes cover;
  `skewCorners` takes a margin over the law's 3° for corners rounded again. The plain metro keeps its cards.
  Its street is the geotown's (`geocity/kit.js` `loud`, any exaggerated cube): its lamps round and lathed, its signals
  on a mast arm with a head at its end and one on its pole, its bins, hydrants and street-name signs the geotown's,
  each a body a loud street inks, never the last of its kind's, and the parts that keep their colours (lenses, glass,
  a sign's face) stamped with it; a loud street's lamps and signals never one height beside the next (`streets.vary`);
  a toon register's pieces each one of three shapes, exaggerated and incongruent, never the last's. And what the
  geotown has that the metro lacked, where it belongs: signs to the traffic on each kerb (a crossing's diamond ahead

  other litter bins (recycling, a solar compactor, a heritage bin, an ashtray bin, a mesh basket, never the last's),
  dumpsters in its midrise courts, donation bins (a clothing drop, a textile bank, a bottle bank) at its parking lots,
  skips on its building sites (`geotown/receptacles.js`, askew in a toon register). A plain metro takes the same added
  pieces in the realistic register, as they are made, in their own colours, at the same places (`kit.js` `piece`);
  its lamps, signals, bins, hydrants and name signs are its own as they were.
  Its parks are the geotown's (`park` in `geocity/scene.js`, from `geotown/sites.js`), on a plain cube and a loud one:
  a park block on its terraces and a park neighbourhood on the land each a path across it each way, a playground on

  their bed's corners; a loud one's bed striped in two greens and its paving in rows of skewed pastel slabs, each on
  the terrace under it.
  Its shore (`shore` in `geocity/scene.js`), on a plain cube and a loud one, where the land met its water as an open
  edge: a quay where the land stands high (its wall down into the water under a coping, a railing along its edge, a

  Its outskirts houses, on a plain cube and a loud one: each half of a semi pair its door at its outer end and its

  Its low-rises and walk-ups, the geotown's flats, on a plain cube and a loud one: windows round all four walls storey

  balcony its own ink, its doors and canopy inks of theirs; a false front over its street wall in a crown never the

  a railing panel, a mooring bollard and a parasol (`kit.js`).
- **A loud street** (`exaggerate.streets`, `geotown/street-loud.js`; the `street` hand carries one), by the shops'
