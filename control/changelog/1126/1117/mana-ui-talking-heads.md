### People talking, drawn in the game's own look

The first step toward talking heads that any game can use: portraits, people talking, scripts played through talk
boxes, or the same scene acted out in the world.

- **The talk box is drawn as part of the game's interface.** In a designed game, a level's scenes now show their lines
  in the same look as the game's menus and HUD, instead of a plain box of their own.
  - The speaker's name is a nameplate on the box.
  - The line types on, and a ▼ shows once it is whole.
  - Choices are numbered, with the one in hand marked.
  - The bar at the foot says which keys do what; a touch screen shows none.
  - The level's HUD steps aside while a scene plays, as it always did on pages without the game's interface.
- **Whoever speaks is shown in the box.** Their own character, head and shoulders, appears live beside their words:
  what they do in the world (standing, breathing, acting a scene) is what the picture shows.
  - A level's speakers sheet can show someone by the box alone, for a scene that is already close on their face.
  - Someone with no body in the level, or the player seen from their own eyes, has no picture.
- **Faces say their lines.** A character with a realistic, an anime or the default (landmark) face now moves their
  face as they speak: their mouth follows the words, their face takes the line's expression (worried, determined and so
  on), and they blink as a speaking face does. The default face opens its jaw on the words. It shows in the world and
  in the talk box's picture. Lines written as a script keep the acting they already had.
- **Recorded voices, if you want them.** A line can carry a recording (a WAV from the voice worker, another speech
  engine or a person). It is saved with the world, the level plays it as the line begins and stops it when the line
  moves on, and the speaker's lips follow the recording. A level can also note which voice each character is cast in.
  Lines without a recording stay silent, as before. A browser plays no sound until the player has clicked or pressed a
  key, so a line that starts before then is picked up partway through.
- **Nothing else changes.** Advancing, holding to skip, Esc and picking a choice by number, arrows or a click work as
  before. A world without the game's interface keeps its box as it was, though its characters' faces now talk too.
