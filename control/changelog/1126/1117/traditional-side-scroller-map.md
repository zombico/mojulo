### The traditional side-scroller map: a level as the camera's path, Galaxy Boy's first

Planned; nothing here has shipped yet. Each line is added as its phase lands.

- **The side strip's seams, mended.** A rolling trail no longer sinks below a strip's ground (its floors lowered to
  boxes upside down), and the joins between a strip's segments are held to a law of their own.
- **A level seen as an elevation.** The course map's sibling: a side-scroller's level as the camera's path through the
  along × up plane, a chain of zones (runs one screen thick, rooms that scroll both ways, full-screen corners), built
  only where the camera can see. Across develops, down is free or a commitment, up is earned.
- **The screen as a measure** beside the hero's reach: what one screen is in metres for a side camera at 16:9, and the
  camera that holds the hero at about an eighth of the frame's height.
- **Going up, earned.** Climbs in their forms (ledges, a switchback, a chimney with rests, a stair, a ladder, pillars
  over a hazard), a fall that costs, light at the top of every climb, a backdrop that knows how high the hero is.
- **Galaxy Boy's first level** laid as a zone chain from its teaching order, ending in its first power; the mascot
  platformer's strip as it was.
- **The map, its laws and its camera**: the elevation drawn with rulers in screens and metres, the laws a chain is held
  to (one screen thick, both walls of a chimney in the frame, rests within three kicks, the next place seen before it is
  reached, the boss's colour its own), and a side camera that holds a zone's locked axis and hands over at a corner.
- **A render benchmark**: a run, a chimney and a boss's room measured on the page by role (the hero's share of the
  frame, the lane's place in it, play and place on opposite hues, recession and sharpness by layer, light from sources
  that are seen).
