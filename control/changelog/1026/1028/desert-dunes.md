### Desert dunes

- **A trail's land laid in sand dunes** (`era/out-dunes.js`, a trail's `ground.dunes: true | { height?, spacing?, wind?,
  repose?, supply?, sinuous? }`). The shape real dunes take, not a noise's: across the wind a dune rises over a long back and
  rounds to its brink, then falls down its slip face at dry sand's angle of repose (`repose`, 32°; so a taller dune's face
  is longer) and eases out at its foot. The crests run across the wind (`wind` in degrees, 0 across the trail), wandering (`sinuous`); where the sand is
  plenty (`supply`) they are long transverse ridges, where it thins they break into crescents whose horns trail downwind.
  Laid over the ground once the walk and the edits are (`era/out-trail.js`), easing to nothing toward the walk (from 1.5 to
  8 m past its edge: the walk keeps a firm inter-dune street and its grade), the far cliff's foot, a pocket and its spur, a
  ford and the site's edges; the mounds lie down where the sand stands. Their sand is cut into the ground along its own
  line through the troughs (`isekai:sand`), no grass on it, no stone placed on it (the site's `clear`). Absent, or
  `false`, adds nothing: the same ground to the byte.
- **The cube's dunes** (`outside.dunes`, the same words): every trail the cube paints takes them, a scene's path and a
  game's levels alike; a trail's own win, its `false` refuses them (`era/geocube.js`, as its water).
- **In words.** "dunes" and "dune" lay the cube's dunes on an open land (no far cliff) in the desert biome; "erg" the
  sea of sand (full supply, 4 m crests); "sandy" a thinner field of crescents. They no longer turn the trail's bumpiness
  up (the mounds would ruffle the sand). "desert" alone keeps its rock desert, no dunes. The geocube manual and the
  `scene` routing card say so.
- **Their sand drawn by the wind, in gen-7** (`scene/channels/form-land.js`, the form read's `dunes`: `era/out-dunes.js`
  duneMap, the wind and a metre map of where the dunes stand and their crests). On the dunes' backs and the flats between,
  ripples run across the wind about 11 cm apart, gentle on their upwind side and steep on their downwind side, their
  spacing wandering over a couple of metres so crests merge and fork, a shade paler at each crest; they fade before they
  alias, and the sand's own tile is calmed under them. A slip face carries none: its sand avalanches in streaks down the
  fall line. A grain in a hundred, a centimetre across, is turned its own way and polished, so the sun catches it as glint
  through the light (lit and shadowed by it, never drawn over it). The shape near the eye needed no more: the land's
  half-metre mesh, shaded smooth, holds a dune's curve and its brink. A land with no dunes carries none of this: the
  same page to the byte.
