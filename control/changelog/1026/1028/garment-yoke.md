### The yoke: cloth on the tiered trunk hangs from the chest, not the shoulder

A jacket, a vest or a loose shirt on the tiered body read as a box over the chest: a shelf at the shoulders, its sides
falling straight past the armpit with the arms tucked under it. Measured on a person and on a beast-kin, the cause is
the trunk's top rings. On the tiered body they are the yoke: they run out into the shoulder balls (a person's at
x 0.348 m, the ball's centre at 0.335). A copy piece (`body-garment.js`) carried each ring out along its own normal, so
the yoke's side stood off sideways past the ball's outer edge (a person's jacket to 0.40 m, a brute beast-kin's 9 cm
past its shoulder). Its drape then held every ring below at least that far out, less a little per metre, so the side
fell from the shoulder point instead of from the chest.

- **The yoke** (`body-garment.js` `yokeOf`, `YOKE_SIDE`). On a trunk with shoulder balls, its rings above the armpit
  (the balls' lowest point) are the yoke. There the cloth stands out sideways by a quarter of its ease at most, so it
  lies on the shoulder with its armhole at the arm's root (a person's jacket at 0.36 m, the shoulder at 0.348). A yoke
  ring holds nothing out below it: the drape begins at the armpit, held by the chest, the bust and the shoulder
  blades. A body without shoulder balls (the classic body), and every piece off the trunk, is cut as before.

  Still open: a trunk piece's ease scales with the trunk's radius (`proportional`, against a limb's 0.1 m), so a very
  wide trunk (a hulking beast-kin) stands its cloth off by some 7 cm and can swallow the top of its arms.
