### Animated face registers and the film look: the eye drawn, lit and moved as an animated character's

The mascot's face is made of lenses that hug its head: they read in a still, but a lid that drops in front of a flat eye
does not read as a round eye closing, and the cel step it is lit by is one look among several. This theme adds head
registers, looks that standardized in 3D animation, routed to by a facet or a brief word rather than a new argument on
any tool, on a rebuilt mascot:

- **the egg mascot**: the whole mascot one skin of eggs. Relations place points in the mascot's frame, an egg sits on
  each (its eggliness the share of its fat end) riding a rig bone, and the eggs blend smoothly into one skin, netted
  along its surface. The skin's weights come from the blend itself: a point belongs to the bones of the eggs it is
  made of, so a joint bends where its eggs meet.

- **globe**: the round eye with its lid on the face. The head is an ellipse and every feature is an egg on it. The eyes
  are eggs standing proud of the face, and a lid is a shell turning about its globe's centre.
- **browed**: the same globes, with the brow attached to the eye as the upper lid's rim, which bends to act.
- **the film look**: soft light on round forms, not a cel step. Smooth blended surfaces, a material per group, a glossy
  cornea whose catchlight is the key light's, and occlusion where masses meet. The lit export carries the materials.

The mouth is the same in every register. New mascots take the new face and look. A stored mascot keeps its own: a
minted one stores its compiled plan, and a stored sheet without the new facets reads as before.
