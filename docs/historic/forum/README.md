# The Forum Romanum: design language

The Forum Romanum is read on **a summer day of 79 CE**, the same moment as the Pompeii scene: Titus's first weeks
after Vespasian's death, after the fire of 64 and the burning of the Capitol in 69, before the fire of 80. It is a
close-scale study beside the Pompeii town: one civic space about 200 × 120 m, walkable at eye height, where the
detail goes into the buildings. It should read as imperial Rome at a glance:
- a long travertine square, open, its ends closed by the Rostra and the Temple of Divus Julius;
- temples on high podia, a screen of fluted Corinthian columns on each front;
- two great basilicas down the long sides, arcade over arcade;
- the Tabularium's arcade and the newly rebuilt Capitoline temple over it at the west, the Palatine at the south-east.

The numbers below are the kit's design language. The machine copy, checked once per principle on the built parts,
is the style card [`control/lib/graph/historic/style/forum.js`](../../../control/lib/graph/historic/style/forum.js);
the parts are [`assets/orders.js`](../../../control/lib/graph/historic/assets/orders.js). When this page and the card
disagree, the card wins. Facts belong in the record (`record/forum.js`), which carries their citations.

What the record settles and what is a design number. The record entry is named in brackets.

## The forum at 79 CE

- **Standing:** Saturn (Plancus's), Concord (Tiberius's), the Tabularium, both basilicas, Castor, Divus Julius, the
  Arch of Augustus, the Regia, the Curia (Augustus's), the Rostra, the Arch of Tiberius.
- **Rebuilt after a fire:** Vesta and the House of the Vestals (after 64), the Capitoline temple (after 69).
- **Not yet:** the Temple of Vespasian (its plot below the Tabularium is open), the Dei Consentes portico in its
  Flavian form, the Equus Domitiani.

## Disputes the scene takes a side on

Each is drawn one way and flagged in the record:
- **Saturn's order in 79:** Corinthian (the standing Ionic porch is late antique).
- **Concord's porch:** six columns (the Tiberian coin), not eight.
- **Divus Julius:** Ionic, after Platner & Ashby and the coins.
- **The Arch of Augustus:** one three-bay arch south of Divus Julius.
- **The Basilica Aemilia:** about 90 m long.
- **Vesta's order:** Corinthian, on the Severan temple's numbers.

## Built

- The orders as parts (`assets/orders.js`): the Attic base, the fluted shaft, the Corinthian, Ionic and Tuscan
  capitals, the entablature run. Each is built once and stands many times as an instance on the World page.
- The buildings (`assets/forum.js`), all placeholders: the podium temple (prostyle or peripteral, a tribunal where
  the record gives one), the round temple, the two-storey arcade bay and the basilica round it, the Tabularium, the
  Rostra, the three-bay and single-bay arches, the Curia, Divus Julius on its platform, plain blocks.
- The square's furniture and the reliefs (`assets/forum.js` monuments; `relief-art.js` skins): statues, rostral
  columns, Ianus's shut shrine, Venus Cloacina, the Lapis Niger, the tribunal, Surdinus's paving letters as written, the
  Curtius relief, the Fasti, the friezes; dedications in real capitals that spell no text (the texts are lost).
- Roofs: tegulae and imbrices, antefixes, ridge tiles, a soffit under the eaves.
- `festival: true` dresses the forum for a procession day: gilded shields on the basilicas' piers, garlands on the
  temple fronts (Livy 9.40.16). An ordinary day shows none.
- The site (`layouts/forum.js`): a measured plan laid out from the record's sizes. The frame's x runs from the
  Tabularium (the real north-west) to the Regia (the real south-east). The positions are the sizes set against each
  other as the plan stands, rounded, not surveyed coordinates, and the ground is level.
