# Mascots: a character read from a promise, not a life

Code: [control/lib/graph/egocube/mascot.js](../control/lib/graph/egocube/mascot.js) (the facets, the brief's words,
the roll) and [control/lib/graph/egocube/mascot-form.js](../control/lib/graph/egocube/mascot-form.js) (the sheet
compiled to a rigged body). The Egocube ([egocube.md](egocube.md)) takes it as its `mascot` tuning: a brief that
asks for a cartoony, mascot-like or "Sonic-like" character rolls a mascot sheet instead of a person's.

The Egocube designs a person: an age, a build, an archetype, a motivation, a life drawn on a face, read in RPG
proportions (seven to eight heads tall) on the hero door or a creature lineage's body. A mascot is a different thing,
not a smaller hero. It is designed backwards from what it stands for and from how it reads at a glance, and nothing
about its body has to be human. This page is the analysis behind the tuning: what the familiar mascots share, the laws
distilled from them, and how each law becomes a facet, a note or a number.

## The laws

1. **A promise, not a life.** A hero is read from a history (age, motivation, wear). A mascot stands for one feeling,
   such as speed, cheer, mischief, might, comfort or a team's pride, and every choice serves it. It has no age band,
   no wear, no shadow side, and it does not age: a fate thread is refused.
2. **The silhouette reads at a glance.** Filled black at 64 px it is still itself: one dominant mass and one hook that
   breaks the outline (the quills, the ears, the snout). If two mascots in a cast fill the same black shape, one of
   them is not designed yet.
3. **Head-heavy, the baby schema.** One to three and a half heads tall, the head a third of the height or more.
   Lorenz's *Kindchenschema*: a big head on a small body, big eyes set low, round cheeks, short thick limbs, little
   or no neck. Appeal comes from this, not from beauty.
4. **The eyes carry the character.** Big (a quarter to two fifths of the head's height), at or below the head's
   middle, tall ovals with a big pupil and one glint. Every other feature is reduced: a dot or a ball for the nose,
   a mouth only when it acts. Eyes come in a few families: a **pair** (Kirby, Mario), **joined** in one mask
   (Sonic), or **dots** (Pikachu).
5. **A round core, and an accent in harmony or in contrast.** The core is always round: round reads safe, friendly
   and huggable. The accent's shape carries the rest of the promise. A round accent is **harmony** (Kirby: circles on
   circles; soft, kind). Spikes are **contrast** (Sonic's round core under its spikes reads as speed and attitude
   held by a friendly centre). Blocks are a sturdy contrast (dependable, strong). The accent's family shapes the hook
   itself: a spiky mascot's ears are points, a round one's are discs, a blocky one's are squared.
6. **Big, medium, small.** Never three similar sizes. The head is the big mass, the body the medium one, and the
   limbs small, roughly 3 : 2 : 1. The extremities break the rule on purpose: thin limbs end in big hands and big
   feet (rubber hose with mitts and shoes), because hands and feet carry the pose.
7. **A palette it owns.** Two or three colours and one accent, saturated and separated in value, so the mascot is
   known by its colour alone. A lighter belly or muzzle patch gives the body a front. In harmony the accent is a
   neighbouring hue (Kirby's red feet on pink); in contrast it is across the wheel (Sonic's red shoes on blue).
8. **Nothing on the surface.** No wear, scars, pores, strands or fur cards: smooth forms a toy maker could cast or
   sew. No part is thin enough to snap off a printed figure or to be cut from a plush. The hook is the only
   complicated form.
9. **A symmetric body with an asymmetric life.** The design is symmetric at rest. The personality is in the motion:
   a cocked head, a tapping foot, a bounce. A mascot's idle is never still, and the way it moves at rest is part of
   the promise (speed taps its foot, cheer bounces, comfort sways, wonder floats).
10. **It only needs to animate.** No skeleton has to be anatomical. A ball with feet, a pear with arms and a biped
    with a big head are all mascots, as long as the rig can bounce, hop, wave and walk them.

## How a mascot differs from a hero

| | The Egocube's person | A mascot |
|---|---|---|
| Read from | a life: what they have done and endured | a promise: the one feeling it stands for |
| Proportion | seven to eight heads (RPG); a child's cast at most | one to three and a half heads |
| Face | structure, expression set by a life, wear | big eyes low on the head, everything else reduced |
| Shape | the role's silhouette (needle, bell, barrel, column) | a round core and one accent family, in harmony or in contrast |
| Colour | ancestry's range, the role's kit, muted by the life | two or three owned colours and an accent |
| Surface | lines, scars, grey, dirt | nothing: toy-smooth |
| Body | human, or a lineage's body on the creature door | any topology that animates |
| Time | ages on a fate thread | never ages |
| At rest | the stance a life has set | always moving: the energy is the idle |

## The mascot sheet

A mascot sheet has the Egocube's form (`notes` and `derives` per channel, the facets, the reads, the given and the
unread words) and its own facets. Each is decided by an explicit facet, a brief word, or the seed, in that order, and
every facet throws its die whether or not it is used.

| Facet | Values | Law |
|---|---|---|
| `promise` | speed, cheer, mischief, might, comfort, pride, wonder | 1: what it stands for; leans every other facet |
| `form` | orb (one head: the head is the body), sprout (about two), toon (about three) | 3, 10 |
| `accent` | round, spike, block | 5: harmony when round, contrast otherwise |
| `eyes` | pair, joined, dots | 4 |
| `hook` | none, ears, quills, snout, tuft, antenna, tail, spines, cap, moustache | 2: the one feature that breaks the outline |
| `extremities` | nubs (stub arms, oval feet), gloves (white gloves, big shoes), paws (mitts and paws in the body's colour) | 6 |
| `energy` | bouncy, zippy, floaty, sturdy, swaying | 9: the idle |
| `hue` | red, orange, yellow, green, teal, blue, purple, pink, brown, white | 7: the body's colour, or a person's clothes |
| `being` | critter, human | a person drawn by the same laws (below) |
| `skin` | porcelain, peach, tan, olive, brown, deep | 7: a person's skin, with its hair |
| `expression` | happy, grin, content, determined, smirk, surprised, sad, angry, sleepy | 4, 9: the face at rest; the clips make the others |

The promise sets the priors of the others. Speed leans toward spikes, the sprout form, joined eyes, gloves and a zippy
idle in blue; comfort toward round, the orb, nubs and a swaying idle in pink. A brief word (`speedy`, `cuddly`,
`spiky`) decides one facet, and a familiar name decides several at once: `sonic-like` is speed, spike, sprout,
joined, quills, gloves, zippy and blue. A name is read as a style; nothing of the named design is copied.

**Faults** are drawn again unless every facet in them was asked for: a soft promise (comfort, cheer) with a spiky
accent, quills on a round accent, a tail or spines that the orb's ball would swallow, gloves on the orb's stubs, and a
snout, a tail, spines or paws on a person.

## Human mascots

A person can be a mascot too: Mario is one. The laws hold unchanged: one to three and a half heads tall, big eyes at
the middle of the head, a round core, big hands and shoes, an owned palette. What changes is what the parts are. The
head is skin; the hair is one chunky form (the head's ellipsoid again, a little larger, set up and back, so it covers
the crown and sinks below the face), squared on a blocky accent and spiked when the hook is quills. A nose stands in the
middle of the face in the accent's family (a ball, a point, a block); ears sit at the sides; the cheeks blush. The hue
goes on the shirt, and the trousers take the accent (contrast) or the shirt's darker self (harmony). A cap is the
person's classic hook: a high crown in the shirt's colour, a bill and an emblem. Brief words: `human`, `person`, `boy`,
`girl`, `kid`, `man`, `woman`; `mario-like` reads as a capped, gloved, red person. The skin is a facet of its own
(`skin`), as given, never derived from anything else.

## The face and its expressions

Law 4 says a mouth is drawn only when it acts. A mascot's mouth acts all the time, so it has one, low on the face (on
the snout when there is one), and the face is rigged:

- **Lids**: a lens of the face's colour over each eye, on its own bone. Open, it is turned up into the head and hidden
  (its pivot is forward of the head's centre, so turning it up sinks it); turned down, it covers the eye.
- **The mouth**: a dark lens with a tongue in it, and two lips of the face's colour over it. The upper lip turned down
  hides the mouth from the top, and what shows is the bowl of the mouth under the lip's arc: a smile. The lower lip
  turned up hides it from the bottom: a frown. Both lips apart make an O, and both meeting make a closed line.
- **Brows**: a bar over each eye that lifts and tilts. They are drawn on a person and on most critters, but not on the
  orb or on dot eyes, where the lids and the mouth carry the face.

Every moving feature turns on a bone (rig chains `lidR`, `lidL`, `lipU`, `lipD`, `browLiftR`, `browLiftL`, `browR`,
`browL`), so an expression is bone motion. It plays in the World's clips and exports in the skinned GLB like the
walk does. The rest face is the sheet's `expression`, built into the geometry, so a still shows it. Each other
expression is a clip of the same name (a body pose with its face), and there is a `blink`. The hop and the cheer change
face as they go. Expressions turn the head only a little, because the face's chains turn in the figure's axes and a
nodding head would slide its lids and lips off the face.

## From a sheet to a body

The sheet compiles to a ring plan on the creature door (`create_character { as: 'creature', spec: { plan } }`), the
same door a lineage's body takes, and so it mints, rigs, plays its clips and exports like any creature. The compiler
holds the laws as numbers:

- **form**: the heads tall, the head's radius and the leg and arm lengths (law 3), on the round core (ring12 at
  superellipse 2, law 5);
- **eyes**: their family, size and height on the head (law 4), each a lens hugging the head with a pupil and a glint,
  and a pale muzzle for the joined mask;
- **accent and hook**: the hook drawn in the accent's family: pointed, round or squared ears, a fan of quills, a ball
  snout, a tuft, an antenna with its bulb, a tail, a row of spines (laws 2 and 5);
- **extremities**: the hands and feet over-sized against thin limbs (law 6);
- **hue and accord**: the body in the hue, a pale belly and muzzle, the accent a neighbour in harmony or a complement
  in contrast, the gloves white (law 7);
- **energy**: the idle clip and the stance (law 9), with a hop and a cheer that every mascot has, and the flavour of
  its walk and run (below, On foot).

Every amount is a first reading for the eyes gate, iterated in place with `update_character` patch ops. The sheet's
ledger says where each note landed.

## The anime style: super-deformed

The laws hold in anime too, but anime SD (super-deformed, chibi) reads several of them its own way. The `style` facet
picks it (`toon`, the western reading above, or `anime`). The words `anime`, `chibi`, `sd`, `super-deformed`,
`kawaii`, `moe` and `manga` ask for it, and the last four ask for a mascot too.

- **S1. Two heads, the head wider than tall:** a bigger head (×1.08, wider ×1.07) on a shorter body; the legs ×0.75,
  the arms ×0.8.
- **S2. Small, soft extremities**, where the toon has big ones: the hands ×0.85, the feet ×0.72, the limbs soft rather
  than rubber hose. This is law 6 read the other way.
- **S3. The eyes are everything.** They are huge, set low (below the head's middle) and wide. A coloured iris fills the
  eye, the pupil sits low in it, a big glint is up and out and a small one down and in, and a heavy lash runs along the
  top. There is no lower lid.
- **S4. Almost nothing else:** a dot for a nose, a tiny mouth tucked close under the eyes, a wide soft blush, thin
  brows set high.
- **S5. A person's hair is locks:** the cap of hair, a fringe of locks over the forehead, a lock down each side of the
  face. The ahoge (the single lock that stands up) is the anime hook; the style leans the hook to it.
- **S6. Pastel:** the colours softened (saturation ×0.78) and lightened, with a soft pink blush.
- **S7. Open, bright faces:** the style leans the rest face to happy, beaming, grinning or surprised. `joy` (beaming:
  the eyes shut in happy arcs, the mouth wide) is an expression and a clip for every mascot, toon or anime.

Any mascot can be anime: a critter, a person, an animal (`a chibi cat mascot`), with any adornment. For an anime hero
in RPG proportions instead, the hero door has its own anime head and chibi build (`create_character({ as: 'hero' })`).
One known gap: a lid turned down over the big anime eye shows as a skin-coloured bump; anime would draw it as the lash
coming down.

## Smooth forms

Law 8 asks for forms a toy maker could cast, and a faceted ball is not one. Every trunk is drawn on twenty-slot rings
(the head on seventeen of them, the body's pear on ten), and every limb, shape and lens on twelve. The rounded shapes
(hands, shoes, ears, muzzles, tails) put their rings densest toward their ends, where a ball turns fastest. The mint
asks for the hull's smooth shading (`hullShade`) under the character light, so the light sweeps the form, not its facets.

## Adornment: one signature piece

A mascot wears one piece, which is the accessory people draw from memory. It is built on the core adornment layer: a
band or shell worn over the body, lifted clear of it by its mugen, with one signature element.

- **Ad1. One piece**, never a costume.
- **Ad2. On a silhouette edge:** the neck, the crown, the waist, the wrists or the back.
- **Ad3. Big and simple**, sized to the head, its signature readable at 64 px.
- **Ad4. It carries the colour the body lacks** (the accent, or for an animal the colour across the wheel from its
  coat), with one gold shine on its signature.
- **Ad5. It says the promise:**
  - speed trails a scarf;
  - cheer wears a bow;
  - mischief knots a bandana;
  - might wears a champion's belt;
  - comfort a bell collar;
  - pride a medal;
  - wonder carries a pack.
- **Ad6. It rides the body:** a rigid piece rides its bone and a ribbon trails. It never covers the face or the hook.

The `adorn` facet holds the piece: none, collar (a bell), scarf (streaming tails), bow (on the crown), belt (a big
buckle), medal (on a ribbon), bandana (its knot's tails down the front), pack (on a band round the body), bands (studded
wristbands). The promise leans it; the orb, with no neck, waist or wrists, wears a bow or nothing. Brief words: `bell`,
`scarf`, `bow`, `belt`, `medal`, `bandana`, `backpack`, `wristbands`.

## The egocube's mascot output: a person made as their mascot

The same character can be made twice: at RPG proportions through the hero door, and as its mascot.
`create_character({ egocube, output: 'mascot' })` does it for one call, and an egocube written with `output: 'mascot'`
does it by default. A person's sheet is not rolled again. Its facets are read into the mascot facets that play the same
part, and the mascot is rolled from them on the person's own seed, so the pair stays one character:

| The person's | The mascot's | Read as |
|---|---|---|
| archetype | promise and hue | its light is the promise (hero: might, jester: mischief, caregiver: comfort, ruler: pride, …), and the colour it owns |
| reading (light, turning, shadow) | expression at rest | light happy, turning determined, shadow smirking (a hero's shadow angry, a sage's light content) |
| role | the piece and the idle | frontline a belt, sturdy; flanker a bandana, zippy; ranged a scarf; caster a medal, floaty; support a pack |
| order | the accent | bound is square, free is spiky, between is round |
| build (a child's age) | the form | lithe and stout the sprout, the rest the toon |
| ancestry | a person's skin, or an animal | the nearest mascot skin; a lineage played by its animal (feline a house cat, lupine a wolf, ursine a bear, avian an owl) or by its mark (sylvan pointed ears, dwarven a moustache) |

What a life wrote (wear, a face set by its years, a kit chosen by a role) has no mascot channel: a mascot does not age,
and its piece is one signature. Each read is recorded with its reason (`mascot.mapping`).

The output carries a **model sheet**, the mascot maker's hand-off. It holds the thesis, the owned swatches by role, what
names it at 64 px, its piece, its rest face and its expression clips, and each of the ten laws with the choice that
answers it. The egocube's preview of a mascot output is the model sheet's **turnaround**: the one character four times,
turned to show its front, three-quarter, side and back.

## Animal mascots

A real species from the fauna roster can be a mascot: `a fox mascot`, `facets: { animal: 'greatHornedOwl' }`. Eight
species were crafted by hand and the rules they shared were distilled into a card per family: three identifiers at
most, stand it up, shorten the face, eyes forward, the coat as flat zones, mass as attitude, the folk reading as the
promise, locomotion as the idle. The analysis, the crafts and the principles are in
[mascot-animals.md](mascot-animals.md).

## Making one

- `create_character({ as: 'mascot', spec: { brief: 'a sonic-like mascot', facets?, seed? } })` rolls the sheet and
  mints its body in one call. The result carries the sheet's facets, reads and unread words.
- To keep the design for many games, roll it as an egocube: `create_sketch({ kind: 'egocube', roll: { brief:
  'something cartoony and spiky' } })` (or `roll: { tuning: 'mascot', facets }`), then `create_character({ egocube })`.
  A brief that asks for a mascot (`mascot`, `cartoony`, `toon`, `platformer`, `plush`, `team mascot`, or a familiar
  name) with no person's facet, years or lineage rolls a mascot; `tuning: 'person'` keeps the Egocube's method. `cute`,
  `kawaii` and `chibi` ask for nothing on their own: a cute knight is a person.
- Revise in place with `update_character` patch ops: `/dials/lean`, `/dials/tilt`, or `/plan/...` for a measure.
- The clips: `idle` (by energy), `hop`, `cheer`, `wave`, `blink`, one per expression (`happy`, `grin`, `content`,
  `determined`, `smirk`, `surprised`, `sad`, `angry`, `sleepy`), and on foot `walk`, `run`, `squat` and `leap`.
- The contact sheet for the eyes gate, from a repo checkout (`control/`):
  `MOJULO_SPIKE_OUT=/abs/dir node ../docs/examples/mascot/render-mascots.mjs 'sonic-like|kirby-like'`, one row per brief:
  the front, the three-quarter and the side at rest, then idle, cheer, the top of the hop and the wave. It is flat-lit
  and diagnostic; the World page's character light is the look. `--faces` draws the faces close up instead: the rest
  face, then every expression.

## On foot

A mascot walks and runs on its own legs, and a game can take it as its pilot.

**The gaits** ([mascot-gait.js](../control/lib/graph/egocube/mascot-gait.js)) are clips on its rig, sized by its legs,
never by a person's. The stride is a share of the leg (a walk 0.9, a run 1.5), cut to what the leg reaches with the root
sunk at most a seventh of the leg in the walk and a quarter in the run. It is centred where the ankle passes under the
hip, because a mascot's big shoe puts its toe well ahead of it. The cycle is a pendulum of that leg: a person's second at
0.9 m of leg, so √(leg / 0.9), never under 0.38 s; the run's is 0.72 of the walk's. A walk keeps a foot down at every key,
both feet down twice a cycle; a run flies twice a cycle, the root rising through the flight. The swing foot lifts high,
as a cartoon step does, but never higher than its leg can fold (a stub thigh cannot draw its foot to its hip). Each key's
root sinks just enough that both legs reach their targets. The arms swing against the legs. The energy flavours it:
bouncy bobs, zippy leans in and trails its arms when it runs, sturdy waddles wide, swaying sways, floaty barely touches
down. `squat` winds a game's jump up and `leap` holds its flight.

**Export.** The rig maps onto a humanoid's: the shin is the lower leg and the metatarsus (from the hock) the foot, as a
game maps a digitigrade leg. So a mascot moulds to the T-pose and exports as a humanoid with its gaits, and so does any
creature-door biped on the same legs.

**As a pilot** ([mascot-pilot.js](../control/lib/graph/egocube/mascot-pilot.js)). A stored mascot is a pilot as a hero
is (`heroRef` names either). It keeps its own size; the game's movement (its speed, its jump, the step it climbs) stays
the game's, as a cartoon hops many times its own height. What follows its body:
- its clips play the runtime's states: forward the run, turn the walk, idle the idle, the jump's squat and leap;
- the stride is the run's travel per cycle at the game's speed, lengthened so the cycle never plays more than four
  times a second (past that its feet skate, as a cartoon's do);
- the capsule is its height and its head's half-width;
- the follow camera is a person's scaled by its height (never under 0.45 of it) and looks at its head;
- a first-person eye sits at its eyes.

A quick game takes one as `quick.hero: { mascot: true | { brief?, facets?, seed? } }`, rolled in the game's setting
(its tradition is the mascot's style) and minted through the creature door, as `create_character { as: 'mascot' }`
mints one. Or adopt one made before: `quick.hero: { ref }`.

**In a sport** ([mascot-kit.js](../control/lib/graph/sportswear/mascot-kit.js)). A mascot plays a sport as any athlete
does, in its own body, and wears the sport's kit as far as its shape has the parts a kit is worn on:
`create_character { as: 'mascot', spec: { brief, kit } }` takes the hero door's kit words (`'soccer'`, or `{ sport,
colours, pattern, trim, socks }`). The kit is paint on the mascot's own parts, as the hero's is on the bare body: the
shirt the trunk's upper part and the sleeves the upper arms, the shorts the trunk's lower part and the thighs, the
socks the shins, the gloves the hands, the patterns and trims as the hero's. An orb wears its shirt as a band low round
its middle, under its face, and its shorts on its legs. Its face, its hook, its adornment and its colours elsewhere stay
its own; a new kit replaces the old one, and `kit: null` takes it off. The kit is recorded on the plan (`mascot.kit`).
A quick sport rolls its athletes as mascots with `quick.athletes: 'mascot'` (each side's in the side's kit, a keeper in
its own), or mixes them into a list with `{ mascot: true | { brief?, facets?, seed? }, kit? }`. On the arena's page a
body shorter than a grown player is drawn at a player's stature, whole (contest/athletes.js `atStature`), and a round
one only as wide as a broad player, so its racquet meets the ball the sim swings at.

Not yet:
- a game's other figures as mascots (its foes, guards and farmhands are people; a sport's athletes may be mascots);
- a mascot hero's hit egg in a shooter (a person's);
- an orb that rolls;
- a mascot's own vault and ledge moves;
- foot IK on slopes at play time.
